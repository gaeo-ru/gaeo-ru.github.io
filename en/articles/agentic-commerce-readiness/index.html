Is your online store ready for AI-agent purchases? 12 checks

Alexey YakovlevPublished: October 22, 2026Updated: October 22, 2026Practical guide

AI assistants already help customers discover products and compare merchants. Some integrations can also facilitate a checkout. This guide explains which merchant information agents need, how programs differ by market, and what an online retailer can prepare now.

A customer asks an AI assistant: “Find an easy-to-maintain espresso machine under my budget, with delivery to my city.” The assistant narrows the options. The next question is commercial: which merchant has the exact model and can fulfill the order?

That second question matters. Product discovery and retailer selection form different parts of the purchase. A merchant needs accurate product data, a reliable offer and facts about the service it provides.

In August, I published an article on Habr examining conversational shopping and protocols for AI agents. This October update addresses operational readiness: 12 things merchants can verify before building or joining an agentic checkout integration.

The short answer

Start with accurate products, stock, pricing and delivery. Then test checkout reliability, authorization and measurement. Evaluate platform-specific integration only after confirming geographic coverage and eligibility.

Three distinct stages of an AI-assisted purchase

1. Product recommendation

The assistant selects a suitable model and describes relevant specifications.

2. Merchant recommendation

The customer sees verified seller conditions, pricing, stock, service and delivery.

3. Authorized checkout

The customer approves the transaction and the merchant confirms the actual order and fulfillment terms.

Checkout adds security and operational requirements: live pricing, safe payment authorization, duplicate-request handling, order changes and returns. Autonomous purchases also require explicit constraints on what an agent may do for its user.

Protocols address different technical tasks. MCP exposes tools and data to assistants; A2A supports communication between agents. ACP, UCP and YCP define commerce workflows within their respective ecosystems. Having an MCP server does not automatically make a store eligible for checkout across every AI platform.

Twelve readiness checks for an online store

Use this checklist with the e-commerce director, marketing team and developers. Record an owner and a reproducible piece of evidence for each check: a product page, sample feed, API response or completed test order.

Catalog and product information

  1. Exact product identity. Each purchasable variant has a stable SKU. If the item uses a GTIN, the identifier is correct.
  2. Useful attributes. Size, compatibility, components and important constraints make like-for-like comparisons possible.
  3. Consistent sources. Product identifier, price, inventory and merchant name agree across the webpage, feed and operating systems.
  4. Crawlable page. The main product facts and purchase terms are available in HTML. Product/Offer structured data represents the actual offer.

Offers, fulfillment and service

  1. Current prices. Currency and payable total are clear before payment. A changed price is communicated and validated.
  2. Reliable stock. The store checks inventory at checkout and responds correctly when an item becomes unavailable.
  3. Shipping and returns. Supported regions, delivery dates and costs, warranty and return terms can be checked beforehand.
  4. Accurate cart totals. The server recalculates items, promotions, delivery fees and final price before accepting an order.

Security and measurement

  1. Buyer authorization. Payment follows the required confirmation of the selected items, address and charge amount. Agent permissions are constrained.
  2. API protections. Requests are authenticated, retries do not produce duplicate orders, and failures are recorded.
  3. Post-purchase support. A buyer can check status, request cancellation under the store's rules and contact support.
  4. Attribution. Product recommendations, merchant mentions, visits, inquiries and paid orders are measured separately.
Turning checks into an implementation plan

For each item, record who owns it and what evidence confirms the current status. Then assign the specific changes needed. This gives the retailer a grounded scope for the next phase.

An initial four-week work sequence

Week 1

Choose priority product categories, collect genuine buyer questions and record baseline AI answers.

Week 2

Check identifiers, stock, price, shipping and return policies for the selected products.

Week 3

Review HTML, Product/Offer, feeds and the reliability of programmatic product and order data.

Week 4

Test the purchase flow, repeat sample questions and confirm the eligibility of relevant partner programs.

This sequence is for an initial audit and remediation plan. Full integration can take longer. Each commerce platform controls its own onboarding process.

Why accurate product information matters for the merchant decision

Some AI answers cite a broad marketplace category page while stating a price for a particular SKU. That does not establish the current offer for the exact product. Other answers choose a merchant based mainly on price because verifiable warranty or support benefits never enter the comparison.

A trustworthy answer needs to match product variants and accurately associate benefits with the merchant that provides them. This creates a concrete task for product teams, marketing and website developers.

How to measure the business result

For online retail, I use five author-defined GEO indicators: PRR for the product recommendation, SCR for merchant criteria, RAR for matching a benefit to the merchant, RRR for retailer recommendations, and PPSR for recommendation stability when the same SKU is cheaper elsewhere.

The GAEO measurement guide explains counting rules. The catalog strategy covers the technical product information. Follow-up measurements should use comparable prompts, dates and interfaces.

Revenue must be assessed using actual store data. A shopper can choose an item in an AI assistant and later place the order directly in a marketplace app. Attribution needs to account for that journey and its blind spots.

Frequently asked questions

Does an online store need its own AI agent immediately?

Most merchants can start by improving product data, stock updates and order reliability. A custom agent is a separate investment, justified by clear buyer use cases and confirmed integration opportunities.

Is Product/Offer markup enough for ChatGPT checkout?

Structured data helps search services understand an offer. OpenAI's partner commerce programs use their own product-feed and integration requirements for supported checkout features.

Does UCP already support every region?

As of October 22, 2026, Google's documented checkout program applies to selected merchants and qualifying products in the US, Canada and Australia. Additional regions must be verified in Google's latest official guidance.

What should an agent check before completing a purchase?

Exact item and variant, quantity, availability, price, shipping costs, applicable terms and the buyer's authorization for the final charge.

How is GEO different from ACP, UCP or YCP integration?

GEO studies product and merchant visibility in AI answers. Commerce protocols support data exchange and checkout operations on specific platforms. Both can rely on the same product catalog.

How can readiness improvements be evaluated?

Compare AI recommendation accuracy, retailer selection and checkout failures. Track actual visits, inquiries and transactions separately, accounting for attribution limits.
