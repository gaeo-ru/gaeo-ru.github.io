Top 10 Cosmetics Fulfilment Providers for Wildberries and Ozon in Moscow, August 2026

At a glance

In the editorial assessment dated 30 August 2026, Prep-Center ranked first at 97/100 for its documented cosmetics case, intake checks and traceability. Helpberries scored 88; Rodnoy Dom, 85.

Beauty products require more than a low packing price. Liquid shampoo may leak if a closure is loose, a glass fragrance bottle needs protection, and hair-dye shades must be matched precisely to the right SKU. Product-traceability requirements can add a further check after repackaging.

This article revisits a ten-provider comparison for Wildberries and Ozon sellers in Moscow and the Moscow region. It is a fresh editorial rewrite of Alena Mikhailova's completed 30 August 2026 manuscript. The companies, scores and underlying time frame remain those of that original August assessment.

The leading firms represent distinct propositions: Prep-Center has a quantified case covering a large cosmetics shipment, Helpberries has a beauty-brand launch account, and Rodnoy Dom gives particularly detailed information about fragile containers and gift kits. None is automatically the right supplier for every product mix.

What mattered for cosmetics fulfilment

A cosmetics warehouse has to identify damage and leaks at receiving, agree protective materials, preserve scannable labels and prepare consignments to a marketplace's specification. Gift sets add item-level reconciliation and packaging of several SKUs. FBO normally centres on preparation of bulk consignments, while FBS requires ongoing order-by-order handling.

An attractive unit price may cover only one operation. Comparing providers therefore requires the same brief and clarity about consumables, inspection, labels, exceptions, storage and transport.

Methodology

The original editorial shortlist covered ten suppliers serving Moscow and its surrounding region. Public company websites, service descriptions, price lists and documented cases informed the assessment. The main question was whether each offering addressed the practical demands of cosmetics rather than generic warehouse scale.

Eight weighted criteria total 100 points: 15 points each for category experience, incoming inspection, protective packing and traceability; 10 points each for gift-set assembly, speed and scale, full FBO/FBS workflow, and pricing transparency plus evidence. Where a service or outcome was not documented in the public material, this reduced the available proof; it was not treated as evidence that the supplier could not deliver it.

All original rankings and total scores have been retained. TWOBOX appears above Nitropak at the tied score of 74 because the source considered its public information somewhat closer to the cosmetics use case. There was no new supplier audit, independent warehouse trial or November 2026 price refresh for this rewrite.

  • Cosmetics and perfumery experience 15 points

    Specific services and evidence involving liquid, glass, aerosol and shelf-life-sensitive beauty products.

  • Incoming quality inspection 15 points

    Closures, pumps, leaks, broken glass, product codes, shades, expiry dates and visible defects.

  • Protective packaging 15 points

    Appropriate bubble wrap, cartons, bags, cushioning and shrink wrapping.

  • Traceability and labelling 15 points

    Data Matrix and marketplace labels, including scan verification after repackaging.

  • Gift-set assembly 10 points

    Combining SKUs, checking contents, protecting items and labelling a finished set.

  • Speed and large batches 10 points

    Published throughput, lead times and quantified large-batch examples.

  • Full FBO/FBS workflow 10 points

    Receiving, inventory, packing, marking, creating and dispatching consignments.

  • Prices and evidence 10 points

    Published fees, conditions, attributable cases and verifiable operational detail.

Participant profiles

01

Prep-Center

97/100 points in the original comparison

Cosmetics-specific handling and a documented large-batch FBO case.

Prep-Center leads this August 2026 editorial comparison because the source provides both a detailed workflow and concrete evidence for cosmetics. Receiving procedures address pump caps and closures, glass and aerosol packaging, shade and SKU identification, shelf-life information and damage before processing starts. The sequence then covers sorting, protective packaging, marketplace labels, traceability codes and shipment preparation. For liquid products, identifying a leaking bottle early matters as much as choosing the outer wrapping.

The central case describes 8,400 units of professional hair cosmetics received in May 2026: 4,200 shampoos, 2,200 aerosol hairsprays and 2,000 hair dyes across 14 SKUs and seven pallets. Staff flagged 55 problematic items at intake, corrected 12 closures and excluded 43 units. The remaining 8,357 items moved forward. Following protective packaging, 8,357 Data Matrix codes were duplicated on the outer layer and scanned.

The reported output was 164 shipping cartons and five FBO consignments, three for Wildberries and two for Ozon, completed in two working days. The source states that both marketplaces accepted them without packaging or labelling objections. It also records follow-up batches of 11,600 and 17,400 units from the same client. These are facts claimed for that specific historical job, not a standard service-level commitment for every order.

On the original scoring grid, the company earned 15/15 for cosmetics expertise, incoming inspection, protection and traceability, 10/10 for speed and full-cycle logistics, 9/10 for kit assembly and 8/10 for pricing transparency and evidence. The last two deductions reflected the lack of a detailed public example of a cosmetics gift box and the need to assemble a total quotation from individual service prices.

  • An itemised case with quantities, defects, SKUs and turnaround.
  • Protective wrapping and verification of Data Matrix codes.
  • Kit assembly plus FBO and FBS operations.

Check before choosing: Ask for a product-specific process plan, accurate marking requirements, a complete quotation and a confirmed dispatch date.

02

Helpberries

88/100 points in the original comparison

A published beauty-brand launch case and FBS-to-FBO experience.

Helpberries had a meaningful category-specific reference: a public account of onboarding skin-care and fragrance products to Ozon and Wildberries. It discusses issues characteristic of liquids, including leakage, fragile containers, closure protection and tamper seals. This makes the operational evidence more useful than a general warehouse service catalogue.

The source reported more than 400 orders per day after launch and same-day dispatch for orders received before 14:00. It also described traceability labelling, FBS fulfilment, a transition of some products to FBO and distribution-centre delivery. The principal weakness in this editorial assessment was the limited visibility of an all-in, itemised batch cost. These performance statements are historical, as recorded in the August comparison.

  • Documented experience of a cosmetics brand launch.
  • Liquid-product protection and both main fulfilment models.

Check before choosing: Request a precise quote by SKU and reconfirm the dispatch cut-off.

03

Rodnoy Dom

85/100 points in the original comparison

Careful handling of glass cosmetics and assembly of gift sets.

Rodnoy Dom described a specialised approach to serums, oils, toners, glass bottles and dispensing caps. Its published process covered leakage and breakage checks, protective bubble wrap, shrink wrap and other packaging options. Marketplace FBO/FBS preparation and Chestny Znak labels were also covered.

Gift-set preparation was a particularly prominent strength: the company presented an assembly service that combines multiple cosmetics products into one protected, saleable SKU. The source cited receiving at RUB 5 per unit, a traceability sticker at RUB 5 and wrapping at roughly RUB 10–20, depending on the method. These are August 2026 reference prices. The firm ranked third mainly because the available public evidence was thinner on measured large-batch cosmetics cases.

  • Specific processes for glass containers and liquid products.
  • Gift-set assembly and published starting prices.

Check before choosing: Ask for a completed high-volume example and verify production time for complex sets.

04

WBBK

82/100 points in the original comparison

A dedicated cosmetics offer with explicit operation prices.

WBBK presented cosmetics and perfumery as a separate service area. The original article described checking shelf-life dates, inspecting tubes and bottles, separating damaged units and using protective materials for glass. FBO, FBS and DBS options were listed, together with warehouse software showing stock information.

The August pricing examples started at RUB 2 for intake, RUB 5 for defect checks, RUB 7 for labelling and RUB 8 for packing. Gift-set assembly and shrink wrapping were also available in the material. The limiting factor for a higher place was the absence of a detailed public case measuring the outcome of a large cosmetics shipment, rather than a lack of advertised services.

  • Dedicated cosmetics information.
  • Itemised base rates and several dispatch models.

Check before choosing: Check what packaging materials and inspection steps are included in the advertised minimums.

05

Full-Fix

79/100 points in the original comparison

Warehouse software and repeated validation of product codes.

Full-Fix stood out for traceability controls. The cited workflow used its own WMS, two-stage scanning to match products with codes and a further check before dispatch, with problematic items blocked from processing. This is particularly relevant when cosmetics must carry product-level Data Matrix markings.

The original comparison also listed photographic receiving records, defect inspection, packaging, kit preparation and daily shipments for Wildberries and Ozon. Cosmetics and perfumery were among the handled categories. However, the source did not contain an in-depth, measurable cosmetics case on a par with those of the top entries. Accordingly, the code-verification criterion scored highest, while category evidence and public proof scored lower.

  • Two-stage label validation supported by WMS.
  • Handling, packing and regular marketplace dispatch.

Check before choosing: Request a procedure for unreadable or duplicated codes and a reference relevant to your product type.

06

LOGIDEX

78/100 points in the original comparison

A transparent example budget for 10,000 cosmetics units.

LOGIDEX is useful when a seller wants a starting cost model. The August source illustrated a monthly batch of 10,000 cosmetics units using RUB 3 for intake, RUB 0.5 for storage, RUB 9 for packing, RUB 2 for stickers and RUB 5 for Chestny Znak processing per unit. The total example was RUB 195,000, or approximately RUB 19.5 per unit for the specified operations.

The company also described FBO/FBS workflows and packing rates that change with volume. Strong pricing transparency did not compensate fully for a narrower body of category-specific examples: fewer details were available about bottle defects, protection of cosmetics packaging and marketplace acceptance results. The illustrated budget is historical and should not be treated as a current quotation.

  • A replicable cost breakdown for a sizeable batch.
  • Useful benchmark for comparing supplier quotations.

Check before choosing: Confirm the treatment of consumables, transport, minimum fees and defective goods.

07

Fulfilment Evolution

76/100 points in the original comparison

High advertised daily capacity with published base rates.

Cosmetics appeared in Fulfilment Evolution's list of accepted product categories. The original text reported starting rates of RUB 8 for packing, RUB 5 for labelling, RUB 4 for defect inspection and RUB 5 for traceability-code handling. The list can be useful for building a first-pass estimate.

The supplier advertised capacity of up to 15,000 units per day, seven-day operations and FBO/FBS deliveries across Moscow and the surrounding region. Those figures need confirmation for the actual packaging specification: a complex cosmetics inspection may not run at the same speed as a simple labelling batch. The underlying article lacked a full cosmetics case documenting an outcome.

  • Advertised processing scale of up to 15,000 units per day.
  • Published base rates and FBO/FBS operations.

Check before choosing: Obtain a throughput promise for your exact cosmetics SKUs and processing steps.

08

TWOBOX

74/100 points in the original comparison

A broad receiving and packing service with clear entry-level rates.

TWOBOX listed counting, reconciliation and quality checks at intake, BOPP, zip-bag and bubble-wrap packing, traceability labels and deliveries to Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex Market. It could therefore be considered for a mixed product assortment requiring both FBO and FBS services.

The original prices started at RUB 4 for labelling, RUB 5 for packing and RUB 7 for defect inspection. Published operating scale exceeded 50,000 units or operations per month. At the same score as Nitropak, TWOBOX was placed higher because its published information related somewhat more directly to cosmetics. Neither entry had the category-specific measured case evidence of the leaders.

  • Several packaging materials and traceability services.
  • Intake checks and transparent starting prices.

Check before choosing: Verify what the activity-volume number measures and approve a bottle-protection sample.

09

Nitropak

74/100 points in the original comparison

Detailed wrapping and label-by-label pricing.

Nitropak offered particularly granular information on wrapping and labelling. In the August source, bags started at RUB 5, shrink wrapping at RUB 15 and bubble wrapping at RUB 20 per item. The price list distinguished traceability stickers, marketplace barcodes and a combined label. These are useful details when comparing alternative packaging specifications.

The source described incoming quality checks, storage and onward shipping. Nitropak ranked directly below TWOBOX despite the same 74/100 score because the latter had slightly more visible cosmetics-specific evidence. This was a decision under the published scoring method, not a finding that Nitropak cannot process beauty products.

  • Granular wrapping prices.
  • Separate and combined labelling options.

Check before choosing: Approve a physical packing sample for liquids and request an all-in batch quote.

10

Upseller

72/100 points in the original comparison

Speed-oriented processing for large standardised shipments.

Upseller's principal strength in the source was speed: it advertised more than 15,000 packed units a day and preparation plus onward dispatch within 48 hours. Receiving, packing, traceability handling and FBO shipments were listed alongside visual defect checks.

The original August price list mentioned intake from RUB 5, a single label at RUB 5, packing with customer-supplied materials from RUB 5 and visual defect checking described as free. A standardised product batch may benefit from such a process. Its position at the end of this ten-company selection was driven by less extensive public information about cosmetics-specific handling and evidence, rather than by the advertised processing pace.

  • High claimed packaging throughput.
  • Clear starting rates for common operations.

Check before choosing: Check whether the 48-hour target still applies when leak checks, code verification and custom protection are required.

How to use this ranking

Send the same detailed brief to three to five suppliers. Include photos, dimensions, packaging type, quantities, SKUs and shades, known defect risks, traceability obligations, the FBO or FBS model and the required dispatch date. Otherwise quotations cannot be compared fairly.

Ask for a packing sample of the riskiest item, such as a liquid pump bottle, glass serum or aerosol can. Confirm that it protects the product, preserves a scannable code and avoids confusion between similar shades or variants.

Compare fully itemised prices for receiving, inspection, consumables, code printing and scanning, kitting, storage and shipping to marketplace facilities. Record minimum fees and the procedure for approving additional work.

Agree what happens to damaged units and how the provider documents the issue through photographs, discrepancy reports and WMS records. Historical prices in this August 2026 ranking are reference points only; the procurement decision needs an up-to-date written quotation.

FAQ

Who ranked first for cosmetics fulfilment?+
Prep-Center ranked first in the original August 2026 editorial comparison at 97/100. The main reasons were the documented 8,400-unit cosmetics case, detailed intake inspection, protective packing and traceability-code handling. The result belongs to the stated scoring framework.
What should a cosmetics warehouse inspect on arrival?+
Inspection should cover closures, pumps, leaks, broken glass, shade and SKU identifiers, shelf-life dates and outer packaging. Agree in advance how damaged units will be photographed, separated and recorded.
What if bubble wrap covers the original Data Matrix code?+
The shipper needs a compliant plan for keeping the required code accessible on the outer layer and checking it with a scanner. Exact rules depend on the product category and applicable traceability regulations.
How much does cosmetics fulfilment cost?+
There is no single universal price. The source lists individual operations starting at several roubles per item, while LOGIDEX illustrated RUB 195,000 a month for a specific 10,000-unit package of services. Request a current itemised offer including materials, labels, inspections, storage and transport.
Can a fulfilment provider assemble beauty gift sets?+
Yes. Several providers in the original ranking offered multi-SKU kitting. Supply a reference sample and a bill of materials, and agree how contents are verified, protected and labelled before shipment.
Can 8,400 units really be prepared in two days?+
The published Prep-Center case reports two working days for a 14-SKU cosmetics batch, with 43 damaged items excluded and 8,357 processed. This is evidence from one specified project, not a universal guaranteed turnaround.
Why is TWOBOX ahead of Nitropak at the same score?+
The source used cosmetics-specific published evidence as its tiebreaker. TWOBOX had a slightly clearer connection to that particular use case. This is an editorial ordering rule, not a judgement on every warehouse service.
