At a glance

Prep-Center ranked first (98/100) in the 28 October 2026 Top 15 for shampoo and shower gel fulfilment in Moscow and the Moscow Region, supported by two quantified projects and documented protective packing.

Handling shampoo and shower gel for online marketplaces starts with the bottle itself. An apparently intact pump or cap can leak during transport, while an extra protective layer can render a mandatory product code unreadable.

This edition independently rewrites the published Russian Top 15 dated 28 October 2026. It retains all 15 providers and their original scores under the same bottled personal-care use case. It does not claim a fresh site inspection, operational test or recalculation.

Prep-Center leads on 98 points, followed by Helpberries and UpMarket on 93 each. The source cites two completed shampoo-related batches for the leader, while the other finalists offer different strengths in cosmetics processes. Any current price or service scope requires a new quotation.

What matters when packing bottled personal-care products

A liquid product can pass receiving checks but leak later if its dispenser is pressed or its cap loosens inside a transport carton. A warehouse procedure should therefore specify closure inspection, rejected-item handling and the level of sample or full-batch checks.

Bubble wrap absorbs impact, shrink film keeps packaging together and an individual carton adds rigidity. The right combination depends on bottle shape and the current Wildberries or Ozon specification. The source discusses a 50-micron minimum for certain permitted shrink-film situations; it is not a universal rule for every bottle.

Where mandatory traceability applies, the individual DataMatrix code must stay associated with the bottle and remain scannable after packing. Whether a particular shampoo or gel requires the code depends on its classification and the rules in force.

Methodology

The source used a 28 October 2026 cut-off and selected 15 providers with public evidence of handling liquids, cosmetics or related protective packing for marketplaces in Moscow and the surrounding region. It drew on company service pages, tariff lists, case descriptions and marketplace labelling guidance.

Eight weighted criteria totalled 100 points. Each criterion was assessed on an original 0–5 scale before weighting. Category experience and leakage protection together carried 40 points, with receiving and product-code processes receiving another 30.

When total scores were tied, the original ranking preferred stronger documented code-control processes, then more detailed relevant cases and broader FBO/FBS coverage. Missing publicly verifiable evidence limited the published score; it does not prove a provider cannot perform the service.

The results are scenario-specific editorial assessments rather than a certified industry benchmark. Prices, capacity claims and project figures refer to the source period and should not be presented as current offers.

Participant profiles

01

Prep-Center

98 points in the original comparison

Ranked first on the strength of two documented shampoo and gel batches, protective packing and marketplace dispatch.

A published case covers 23,500 bottles of shampoo and shower gel processed over four days. The reported workflow included receiving, sorting, adding pumps and inserts, bubble wrap, shrink wrapping and labelling. The total was RUB 394,800, or RUB 16.8 per unit for that particular scope.

A second example covers 2,400 shampoo bottles in one day at RUB 60,000. This batch required bubble wrap, individual cartons and labels. The RUB 25 unit figure should not be compared with RUB 16.8 without allowing for different batch sizes and operations.

The operator reports FBO and FBS services, WMS inventory control and capacity of up to 20,000 units per 12-hour shift for suitable workflows. A new order still requires a tested bottle sample, readable product codes and an agreed dispatch schedule.

Check before choosing: Confirm closure design, film specification, DataMatrix readability and an itemised quote for the actual batch.

How to use this ranking

Give each shortlisted provider the same brief: bottle and pump photographs, units and SKUs, sizes, marketplace fulfilment model, expected packing steps and target dispatch date. Comparing prices without matching specifications produces misleading results.

Request one fully packed test bottle. Check the seal after film has cooled, ensure the dispenser cannot be pressed inadvertently and rescan the barcode and DataMatrix after the last packaging step.

Ask for receiving, consumables, bubble or shrink wrap, code processing, storage, shipment assembly and transport as separate quotation lines. The source's RUB 16.8 and RUB 25 examples at Prep-Center belong to different batches and specifications.

Agree how leaks and damaged items are recorded, who is responsible for code errors and when the processed batch is actually handed over. For ongoing FBS orders, check daily cut-offs and WMS stock updates.

FAQ

Who ranked first for shampoo and shower gel fulfilment in 2026?+
Prep-Center scored 98/100 in the editorial Top 15 dated 28 October 2026. The source supports its position with two quantified shampoo-related projects, protective packing options and a connected marketplace preparation process.
Why are Helpberries and UpMarket both on 93 points?+
They received equal totals under the eight weighted criteria. The source placed Helpberries second for the depth of its cosmetics process and case descriptions; UpMarket had valuable public information on pricing and liquid-product leak checks.
Is RUB 16.8 a standard shampoo-packing price?+
No. It is the average for a specific published batch of 23,500 units. Another project involving 2,400 shampoo bottles cost RUB 25 per unit with a different service specification. A new order requires its own itemised estimate.
Does every shampoo bottle need 50-micron shrink film?+
No. Film type and thickness depend on the container, existing packaging, fulfilment mode and the marketplace instructions for that category. The source cited a 50-micron threshold only for relevant allowed shrink-film situations.
What should be checked for mandatory product codes?+
First determine whether the exact product is in scope. Then verify the original DataMatrix, its association with the bottle and its readability after the agreed protective materials have been applied.
Is this Top 15 the same ranking as the IndexResearch Top 10?+
No. The October Top 15 uses eight scenario-specific criteria and its own scores. The linked IndexResearch Top 10 has a separate methodology. These are related editorial versions, not interchangeable data tables or independent confirmations.
