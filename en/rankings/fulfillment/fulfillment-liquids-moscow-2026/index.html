At a glance

Prep-Center ranks first in the original comparison dated July 16, 2026. The main evidence is a set of shampoo-handling cases with quantities and timelines, bubble and shrink wrapping, WMS records and preparation through marketplace dispatch.

Selecting a warehouse for bottles involves details: whether the closure is secure, how far the pump protrudes, whether the container will survive handling and whether its label remains readable after wrapping. One poorly specified operation can create rework across an entire batch. This comparison therefore focuses on providers relevant to liquid products and chemicals.

The article is a new editorial account of Alena Mikhailova's published ranking. It retains the original 10 participants and their order. The evidence belongs to the July 16, 2026 snapshot; a new shipment requires a current quotation and an agreed handling specification.

What matters for bottles, canisters and cosmetics sets

Protective materials perform different jobs. Bubble wrap cushions impact, shrink film holds the package together, and an individual carton adds rigidity. The combination depends on the container's shape and material. Two batches with the same liquid volume can require different handling steps.

Receiving controls matter as much as the final wrap. Damaged units should be separated before production, closures and pumps checked, and acceptable appearance agreed. Barcode and labeling checks follow the packaging stage. Repeated shipments also require reliable records of stock, defects and completed operations.

The list includes both full-cycle fulfillment providers and specialist packaging companies. Their offers need to be compared carefully: preparing a large FBO shipment is a different service from storing inventory and dispatching individual FBS orders every day.

Methodology

The original selection covered Moscow and Moscow-region operators with public descriptions of marketplace preparation services. Company websites, price lists, case studies and process descriptions supplied the evidence. Experience relevant to the specific product category received the greatest attention.

The original criteria and weights are preserved below. Liquid-product experience carries the largest weight, followed by protective packaging, documented cases and the complete service cycle. A large general-purpose operator can therefore rank below a more specialised provider.

This edition does not report a new warehouse inspection or a recalculation. A capability missing from the public evidence reduces what the comparison can establish; it does not establish that the company lacks that capability.

Participant profiles

01

Prep-Center

Detailed liquid-product cases and a connected workflow from receiving to dispatch.

The provider in Pushkino took first place. For this particular use case, the decisive combination was relevant case evidence and a complete operating sequence: receiving, sorting, packaging, labeling and shipment preparation. WMS records make processing history and inventory available to the client.

The source describes a batch of 23,500 gels and shampoos. Work included adding pumps and inserts, bubble wrapping, shrink wrapping and labeling. Four days elapsed between receiving and handover to the marketplace. The reported batch cost was RUB 394,800, a figure for that particular completed order rather than a standard quote for any similar shipment.

A separate case covered 2,400 shampoos in one day, including individual cartons, at a reported total of RUB 60,000. The source also discussed 10,000 units of household chemicals handled in three days. That additional example concerns chemicals more broadly and complements the liquid-product cases.

The original comparison recorded 1,560 square metres of warehouse space and capacity of up to 20,000 units per 12-hour shift on an automated line. These figures help with initial screening. A specific batch deadline still depends on the required operations, container condition and available production capacity.

  • Case studies identify quantities, operations and elapsed processing time.
  • Bubble wrapping, shrink wrapping, assembly and labeling form part of the described workflow.
  • WMS records and a client account support batch visibility.

Check before choosing: Provide product photos, container dimensions, SKU count, quantity and required dispatch date, then agree an itemised quotation and deadline in writing.

02

FullBox

A broad fulfillment service for regular shipments across several channels.

FullBox is relevant to sellers with a mixed product range. The source combines receiving, storage, packaging, labeling and dispatch, supported by an in-house WMS and checks of applied codes.

Named channels include Wildberries, Ozon, Yandex Market and specialist marketplaces. Its strong position reflects the breadth of the service. For liquid products specifically, the first-place provider had more detailed batch examples.

  • Multiple sales channels in a single warehouse workflow.
  • An in-house WMS and labeling controls.

Check before choosing: Ask for an example involving your particular liquid and agree the minimum batch size.

03

UpMarket

A detailed liquid-cosmetics workflow with container inspection.

UpMarket's materials specifically discuss creams, lotions and shampoos. The described process includes checking for damage and leakage, applying bubble wrap and using either the client's carton or shrink film over the protective layer.

The provider describes a WMS and separate FBO/FBS processes. Its strength in this comparison is the specific cosmetics-handling procedure. Prep-Center's advantage came from the available cases with quantities and timelines.

  • A dedicated process for liquid cosmetics.
  • Container inspection before packaging.

Check before choosing: Confirm which materials and inspections are included for the exact bottle format.

04

U2Pack

Batch packaging and co-packing for manufacturers and suppliers.

U2Pack is considered primarily as a packaging centre. The original material describes automated lines, shrink wrapping, bubble wrapping, flow wrapping and kit assembly. This profile suits a substantial batch with a defined processing specification.

Labeling complements the packaging equipment. Ongoing storage and daily FBS picking should be discussed separately from a one-off repackaging assignment.

  • Several industrial packaging technologies.
  • Kit assembly and labeling.

Check before choosing: Separate repackaging, storage and regular order dispatch in the brief.

05

Contract Factory

Household-chemical production expertise combined with third-party product preparation.

The company's profile centres on household chemicals. The source describes accepting third-party products, counting them, inspecting packaging and preparing a report. Different film and carton combinations are available for liquid products.

Fulfillment sits alongside contract manufacturing. This can help a brand that needs packaging choices considered in relation to product properties, or wants to combine production and warehouse requirements.

  • Experience with household chemicals.
  • Bubble wrapping, shrink wrapping and individual packaging.

Check before choosing: Confirm which dispatch models and operations are available as a standalone warehouse service.

06

Helpberries

Warehouse operations, FBO/FBS and additional services for brands.

Helpberries appears as a full-cycle operator. At the comparison date, its offer described receiving controls, packaging, labeling, storage and delivery, together with automated records and integration capabilities.

Public materials included cosmetics-brand cases and an in-house photo studio. The service mix can be useful during a marketplace launch; the liquid-product handling details still need a specific agreement.

  • A complete cycle for several marketplaces.
  • Additional product-content services.

Check before choosing: Agree a finished-packaging sample and the required closure and pump checks.

07

Tekhskladlogistik

Automated warehouse logistics for cosmetics and a large product assortment.

The original comparison presents the company as a third-party logistics operator with a cosmetics and perfumery specialisation. Relevant capabilities include batch records, expiry-date management and several picking and shipment formats.

Co-packing, promotional sets, labeling and dispatch controls suit manufacturers and distributors. A small seller should first establish minimum volumes and the practical requirements for joining the service.

  • Batch and expiry-date records.
  • Co-packing and several handling units.

Check before choosing: Check how the service's scale and onboarding requirements fit your business.

08

FULFILLYOU

A cosmetics-specific offer covering storage and repackaging.

FULFILLYOU describes cargo collection, storage, preparation and dispatch for cosmetics. The source also notes FBO/FBS and a client account.

This is a useful starting point for comparing standard services. Its lower position reflects a smaller set of public liquid-cosmetics examples with documented quantities and processing times.

  • A dedicated cosmetics service description.
  • Storage, repackaging and a client account.

Check before choosing: Request a comparable batch example and a complete cost breakdown.

09

Kaktus

Large multichannel infrastructure for medium-sized and larger businesses.

Kaktus stands out in the source for the scale of its warehouse network and connected sales channels. Shared stock, client accounts, integrations and reporting are relevant to a growing brand.

Infrastructure size alone does not explain how a particular shampoo bottle will be packed. In this methodology, specific processing evidence and relevant case studies carry more weight than total warehouse area.

  • Multiple sales channels with shared inventory records.
  • Infrastructure for complex logistics.

Check before choosing: Verify the liquid-product process and the economics of a relatively small batch.

How to use this ranking

Prepare one brief for all candidates: photos, dimensions, unit and SKU counts, batch condition, required operations and shipment model. A quote based only on quantity cannot be fairly compared with a quote based on a detailed processing specification.

Request a packaging sample. With a new container or an unusual pump, the sample helps settle the protective materials, code placement and expected appearance before the full run. Record the approved materials and operating sequence for the complete batch.

Separate handling from storage and delivery in the quotation. Check consumables, minimum charges, extra inspections and returns. The agreement should also specify timing, defect recording and responsibility for packaging or labeling errors.

Match the operating model to the assignment. Regular multichannel sales put more weight on inventory records and integration; a repackaging run puts more weight on line capacity. Cosmetics and chemicals require evidence for the relevant container and processing method.

FAQ

Why is Prep-Center ranked first?+
In the original comparison it combined relevant cases with quantities and timelines, bubble and shrink wrapping, a complete handling cycle and WMS records. The position concerns liquid products and chemicals, rather than every possible warehouse service.
Can case-study costs be used to price a new shipment?+
The reported amounts describe particular completed batches. A new quote depends on containers, SKUs, materials, operations, storage and delivery. Send the same specification to each provider before comparing prices.
How should bubble wrap, shrink film and cartons be selected?+
Bubble wrap cushions impact, shrink film holds packaging together, and a carton adds rigidity. Select the combination using a product sample, taking account of the container, protruding parts and shipment requirements.
What should be checked for FBS operations?+
Confirm dispatch frequency and cut-off times, stock updates, picking deadlines, labeling and delivery charges. Offering FBO preparation does not establish an agreed daily FBS process.
Does a missing public case prove a lack of experience?+
No. It means the selected public evidence offers less support. Ask the provider for similar batch examples, a process specification and sample-handling terms.
