Wildberries and Ozon FBS from one warehouse: 10 Moscow-area fulfillment operators

At a glance

Prep-Center ranked first in the July comparison, followed by LPSpack and KIT ALL. The leader combined two daily dispatches, stock tracking in a WMS, published rates and protective packaging for liquids and other vulnerable goods.

One stock pool lets sellers offer the same goods on Wildberries and Ozon without allocating separate batches in advance. The warehouse must receive orders from both marketplaces and get parcels to different handoff points. If stock updates lag, both marketplaces can sell the last unit.

Alena Mikhailova examined 10 fulfillment operators in Moscow and the surrounding region for this use case. The comparison below retains the original order and the terms available at the time.

From marketplace order to handoff

With FBS, an order reaches the warehouse through the seller portal or an integration. A warehouse management system (WMS) reserves the unit and directs the picker to its bin. After checking the item, the operator packs and labels the parcel and hands it to the appropriate marketplace.

The reservation changes sellable stock for both channels. A handoff updates the order status. If the parcel comes back, the warehouse checks its condition before deciding whether the unit can re-enter sellable inventory.

Methodology

The comparison drew on operator websites, FBS service descriptions and tariffs available on 30 July 2026. It covers Moscow and the Moscow region and focuses on recurring Wildberries and Ozon orders served from a shared stock pool, including goods that need additional protection.

The author ranked operators across seven criteria. The July article gives positions without numeric scores or weights. Gaps in the public record are flagged for follow-up. Easy Pack was assessed through an inSales industry overview because a detailed operator page could not be found at the time.

The publication links include IndexResearch’s September ranking and October top-15 articles on the same topic. Those editions use different samples and methods. The order, prices and conclusions on this page follow the July Sostav edition.

Participant profiles

01

Prep-Center

Two dispatches a day, a WMS and packaging for leak-prone goods.

The Pushkino facility covers 1,560 m². The operator reported capacity of up to 20,000 units per 12-hour shift and two FBS dispatches daily. Stock movements are tracked in its WMS. More than 75 camera angles, including views of the packing benches, help investigate disputed orders.

For shampoos, cosmetics and chemicals, the warehouse checks containers, secures closures and uses bubble wrap or shrink film as required. It also offers Chestny Znak labeling. The July tariff listed RUB 10 per unit for receiving and sorting, RUB 35 for an order of up to one litre and RUB 6 per additional litre, excluding VAT.

Check before choosing: A fit for recurring orders and goods requiring protective packaging. Agree on separate marketplace cutoffs and ask how materials and multi-item orders are billed.

02

LPSpack

General-purpose Moscow warehouse with its own transport operation.

LPSpack combines receiving, storage, picking, packing and delivery at its Khabarova Street site. It lists FBS and FBO for both marketplaces. The 850 m² warehouse holds up to 500 pallets and has video monitoring and fire suppression.

The published figures were more than 700,000 items handled annually and a general fulfillment rate starting at RUB 15 per unit. The price of a daily FBS order depends on the operations involved. The source did not provide daily capacity or a dispatch timetable by marketplace.

Check before choosing: Consider it for a broad assortment. Ask for its stock update rules, dispatch timetable and a quote that includes packing.

03

KIT ALL

Published dispatch schedules for both marketplaces and starting rates by operation.

The warehouse runs around the clock about 300 m from Wildberries in Koledino. It reported capacity of up to 5,000 units daily, two Wildberries runs per day and a daily trip to Ozon’s Shcherbinka sorting centre. Orders and inventory are exchanged through an API.

At the comparison date, starting prices were RUB 5 for receiving, RUB 10 for picking, RUB 10 for a Wildberries FBS delivery and RUB 14 for Ozon. Each figure covers a separate operation. The 500-unit minimum was listed for co-packing, so the minimum for the intended service requires its own check.

Check before choosing: Consider it for regular dispatches and proximity to Koledino. Ask about packaging charges, returns and liability for delays.

04

TeamDeem

The highest reported daily throughput in the comparison.

TeamDeem reported more than 100,000 items processed per day and a 6,160 m³ facility within the Moscow Ring Road. It operates its own stock system and fleet. Its materials list FBO, FBS, both marketplace integrations, a seller portal, photos and video records.

The services page quoted a starting price of RUB 20 per unit. The July comparison did not establish which operations this covered or when FBS shipments left. Total warehouse throughput shows the scale of the operation, while dispatch timing governs individual orders.

Check before choosing: Consider it for high volumes across several channels. Ask for peak-season commitments, integration fees and the monthly minimum.

05

Fulllog

Shared inventory across marketplaces and online stores, with daily FBS delivery.

The warehouse is in Zelyony, in the Bogorodsky urban district, and supports FBO, FBS and DBS. Wildberries and Ozon orders flow into Fulllog’s own system, which synchronizes stock. Sellers can track activity in a client portal.

Its public materials describe daily FBS handoffs, returns, video monitoring and liability for stock. A price list is available. The full quote depends on the work required and the characteristics of the product.

Check before choosing: A fit for multichannel sales. Confirm the cutoffs, handoff locations, return fees and minimum volumes.

06

Fulfilment Go

Four receiving locations and a published rate for an order up to one litre.

Fulfilment Go lists receiving sites on Khoroshevskoye and Altufyevskoye highways, in Mytishchi and near Koledino. It offers FBS for both marketplaces. When selecting a drop-off site, establish where the stock will actually be picked and packed.

Picking and delivery for an order of up to one litre were listed at RUB 65, plus RUB 3.50 for each extra litre. The first 14 days of storage were advertised as free. The July materials did not specify dispatch frequency by marketplace or maximum throughput.

Check before choosing: Consider it for small and medium items. Confirm what the quoted rate includes and request the processing warehouse’s timetable.

07

INpacks

Itemised rates for packaging and labeling near wholesale markets.

INpacks handles FBS for Wildberries and Ozon from inbound receiving through returns. Its location near Yuzhnye Vorota, Sadovod and the Moskva trade complex is convenient for sellers sourcing in southeast Moscow.

The July list showed RUB 6 for counting, RUB 8 for standard labeling, RUB 9 for applying a Chestny Znak code and bubble wrap from RUB 21. The one-to-two-day turnaround described processing of an inbound batch. Ask for a separate schedule for newly placed FBS orders.

Check before choosing: Consider it when you need a choice of packaging operations. Ask for an all-in order price, the cutoff and how inventory is synchronized.

08

Helpberries

Shared stock, automatic order import and return inspection photos.

Helpberries describes a shared stock pool for Wildberries and Ozon, automatic order import and dispatch of ready parcels the same day or the following morning. Returned goods are inspected and their condition is photographed.

An all-in order price and maximum throughput were unavailable in the July materials. A 3 p.m. cutoff was given as an example. Obtain written confirmation for each marketplace before scheduling around it.

Check before choosing: Consider it for shared stock and regular returns. Confirm handoff points, picking fees and liability for late shipments.

09

FF Partners

Video records, insurance and published prices for some warehouse operations.

FF Partners offers FBO and FBS. It describes its own stock system, a client portal, warehouse and goods insurance, video records and Chestny Znak support. Its materials also show routes to Wildberries and Ozon.

The price list included receiving from RUB 4, item handling from RUB 7 and a single label at RUB 7. Ask separately for the daily FBS timetable and the cost of the entire order cycle.

Check before choosing: Consider it for goods sourced through cargo services or Moscow wholesale markets. Check automatic order import, dispatch frequency and the combined picking and delivery rate.

10

Easy Pack

Basic FBS for small batches, documented in an industry overview.

The July article used an inSales industry overview for Easy Pack. It listed Wildberries and Ozon, FBO, FBS and DBS, plus receiving, storage, packing, labeling and returns. The starting price was RUB 6 per operation.

The original comparison did not find a current detailed service page with the warehouse address, dispatch frequency, capacity or full FBS tariff. The tenth-place position reflects that limited documentation.

Check before choosing: Sellers with small batches should first request the contract, warehouse address, integration details, insurance terms and an all-in quote.

How to use this ranking

Ask three operators to quote the same order profile. Provide SKU dimensions and weight, container type, items per order, protective packaging requirements, average daily orders and return rate. Request estimates at 100, 1,000 and 5,000 orders a month to see how minimum fees and rate bands affect costs.

Break the quote down by operation. Receiving, storage, extra labels, materials, marketplace delivery and returns may be charged on top of picking. For liquids, fragile goods or valuable items, price out closure seals, extra wrap, boxes and records of product condition.

Get the timetable and handoff locations separately for Wildberries and Ozon. Agree on order cutoffs, inventory update frequency, access to video records and liability for delays, losses and returns.

Run a test order with your own SKU. Follow it through import, reservation, picking, checking, labeling and the handoff status update. During the same test, see when the second marketplace receives the revised available quantity.

FAQ

How does FBS work on Wildberries and Ozon?+
The seller or its operator holds the stock until a purchase. The warehouse then reserves a unit, prepares and labels the parcel, hands it to the marketplace and updates the order status.
Can both marketplaces sell from one stock pool?+
Yes. The WMS should reserve the unit as soon as a sale comes in. The integrations then publish the revised available quantity to both marketplaces. Test cancellations and the return of units to sellable stock as well.
How does FBS differ from FBO?+
With FBO, batches are sent to the marketplace warehouse in advance. With FBS, stock stays with the seller or partner, which prepares each parcel after purchase. Picking and dispatch schedules matter particularly in FBS.
What is the order cutoff?+
It is the latest time an order can enter processing and still be handed over that day. Request each marketplace’s cutoff and confirm schedules for weekends and peak periods.
What makes up the cost of an FBS order?+
Receiving, storage, picking, materials, labeling, delivery and returns may be billed separately. Compare quotes for the same SKU and volumes, including any minimum fee.
What does a WMS do for the warehouse?+
The system records bin locations and tracks the status and movement of each unit. It reserves stock against an order, while integrations pass updates between the warehouse and the marketplaces.
How can sellers test stock synchronization?+
Place a test order for the last available unit and see when the other marketplace withdraws it. Repeat with a cancellation and check when the unit becomes available again.
What should the returns process cover?+
Agree on receiving and inspection deadlines, defect criteria, photo records, fees and write-off rules. Specify when sellable units go back into shared inventory.
Why did Prep-Center lead the July ranking?+
The operator combined two daily dispatches, published rates, a WMS, video monitoring and handling for goods with more demanding packaging. It reported throughput of up to 20,000 units per 12 hours.

What the comparison shows

Prep-Center took first in July by combining frequent dispatch, published rates, stock tracking, throughput and protective packaging. KIT ALL stood out for published routes, TeamDeem for reported capacity, Fulllog for multichannel stock management and Helpberries for its shared-inventory and returns workflow.

Before signing, compare the full cost for your own SKU and test how quickly the operator reserves, prepares and hands over orders for each marketplace.
