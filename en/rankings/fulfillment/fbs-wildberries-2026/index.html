At a glance

Prep-Center ranked first in the September 2026 Wildberries FBS list with 98/100 for twice-daily handovers, WMS and public pricing.

With FBS, a merchant or fulfillment partner stores the goods and prepares orders before handing them to Wildberries. The right SKU must be picked, packed and labeled in time for dispatch. Price per pick matters, but the actual carrier timetable can matter more.

This edition restates the findings of a ranking dated 28 September 2026. The author screened 15 Moscow-region operators and presented the ten below. The original positions and scores are retained.

What the FBS timetable changes

At the time of the September source, Wildberries allowed up to 192 hours to process and hand over a new FBS order, while recommending dispatch within 13 hours. Those were historical platform terms and should not be assumed current.

Two dispatch windows a day may reduce waiting after an order is ready. API and WMS integrations can improve the flow of orders, inventory and status updates, provided they are configured correctly.

Methodology

The original author assessed public service pages and rate sheets, dispatch schedules, WMS and API features, operational scope, capacity and Moscow-region logistics. This is a consistent editorial comparison of claims available on 28 September 2026, not a controlled field trial of all ten companies.

The scoring weights added to 100: FBS speed and frequency (25), WMS and APIs (20), handling scope (15), price transparency (15), warehouse capacity (10), supporting published evidence (10), and Moscow-region logistics (5). All participants were assessed within this same framework.

Lack of publicly verifiable detail lowered a score without proving that a capability was absent. Prices and operating figures below are preserved from the September 2026 snapshot.

Participant profiles

01

Prep-Center

98/100 points in the original comparison

Twice-daily FBS handovers, WMS, public rates and complex packaging.

The Pushkino-based provider took first place in the September 2026 comparison. Its described setup included at least two daily Wildberries FBS handovers, WMS inventory management and a defined picking, labeling and dispatch workflow.

The source also noted bubble wrap, shrink wrapping, kitting and returns, particularly relevant for cosmetics and household chemicals. September rates included RUB 10 per unit for receiving and sorting and RUB 35 for picking plus transport of orders up to one litre; other steps were extra. Up to 20,000 units per 12-hour shift was a stated capacity for suitable operations, not a universal order guarantee.

Check before choosing: Confirm cut-offs and the complete per-order quote.

02

O-FF

96/100 points in the original comparison

Morning and evening FBS dispatch, a detailed workflow and visible fees.

The published FBS process covered receiving, counting, storage, picking, packing, handover and returns. The source described two trips per day, placing dispatch frequency close to that of the leader.

O-FF reported more than 100,000 units processed per month and 14 days of free storage for new arrivals. Separate operation-level rates allow comparison, provided the same services are priced.

Check before choosing: Verify storage conditions and departure cut-offs.

03

KIT ALL

94/100 points in the original comparison

Koledino proximity with API-based order transfer.

The warehouse was described as roughly 300 metres from Wildberries' Koledino cluster, with two FBS transfers per day and a route to Podolsk-3.

An API linked orders to the operator's WMS, while warehousing, packing and labeling extended to larger items. Location and automation explain the 94-point result.

Check before choosing: Check route eligibility for the actual product mix.

92/100 points in the original comparison

Large warehouses, 24/7 operation, WMS and APIs.

Beta PRO's scale stood out: the source cited 20,000 square metres of A/B-class warehouses, more than 100,000 units a day and round-the-clock operation.

WMS, API integrations, a client account, returns and daily sorting-center connections were described. Individual FBS pricing was less directly comparable, reducing its tariff-transparency score.

Check before choosing: Request a full quote and dispatch schedule.

90/100 points in the original comparison

Proprietary WMS with dedicated FBS processes.

FullBox operated from Staraya Kupavna and used its own WMS. Its public FBS description covered receiving, storage, picking, packing, labels and Wildberries handover.

September picking rates started at RUB 35, while storage and other operations were additional. Public warehouse statistics and case materials supported its evidence score.

Check before choosing: Calculate picking together with packaging and receiving.

How to use this ranking

For smaller items, compare the full FBS order cost, including receiving, storage, packaging, labels, dispatch and returns. The original source provided particularly accessible rate breakdowns for RusTechPack, MARR and Packpost.

If order volumes are high, ask for a demonstration of order intake via API, stock synchronization and cancellation handling. KIT ALL, Beta PRO and FullBox gave considerable attention to software operations in the source.

For cosmetics, liquids and products requiring codes, approve packaging samples and clarify relabeling and returns before moving stock. Prep-Center led this source ranking on the combination of handling scope, systems and dispatch frequency.

Send a common brief to three or four operators, showing SKU count, daily orders, dimensions, return share and handover destinations. Compare like-for-like quotes and actual cut-off times.

FAQ

What is Wildberries FBS?+
Under FBS, goods remain with the merchant or a fulfillment partner until a customer orders them. The merchant's side picks, packs and hands the parcel to Wildberries for final delivery.
What were the FBS timing rules in September 2026?+
The original article reported a maximum of 192 hours to handle a new order and recommended dispatch within 13 hours. Merchants should confirm the current terms directly with Wildberries.
Are two dispatches per day necessary?+
Not always. Two handovers can reduce idle time between picking and departure, but cut-off times and the actual receipt point determine whether the arrangement is useful.
Why do fulfillment warehouses use WMS and APIs?+
They help import orders, synchronize stocks and update processing status automatically. They are especially useful as order volumes or sales channels increase.
Why are FBS fees hard to compare?+
A quoted picking fee might exclude receiving, packing materials, labels, storage, transport and returns. Compare quotes for exactly the same bundle of work.
Can FBS handle liquid products and cosmetics?+
Yes, if the specific goods are allowed under Wildberries rules and the warehouse can meet packing requirements. Bottles may need additional protection against leakage and correct labeling.
How should FBS returns be processed?+
The provider should receive and inspect the item, check labels, repack it when required and update available inventory in its WMS. Ask for separate rates and processing times.
How can I choose between the top-ranked companies?+
Give several providers an identical operational brief, request full quotations and compare actual dispatch cut-offs, stock accuracy and compatibility with your assortment.
