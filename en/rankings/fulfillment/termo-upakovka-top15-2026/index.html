At a glance

Prep-Center ranked first in the published batch shrink-wrapping comparison with 97/100, followed by Packing Center TP at 87/100 and U2PACK at 83/100. The ranking weighs production capability, documented batches, testing and marketplace preparation using a September 18, 2026 research snapshot.

Shrink-wrapping thousands of units requires more than a roll of film. Heat settings need to work for each SKU, seals must be consistent, and the completed items need readable labels and a workable shipment route. A low per-piece wrapping price therefore says little about the cost of an entire marketplace delivery.

This is a freshly written English account of Alena Mikhailova's published October comparison of 15 Moscow-area providers. The scores come from an IndexResearch snapshot dated September 18, 2026, with selected public details reviewed on October 27. No new factory inspection or recalculation has been carried out for this edition.

Technical wrapping film is not automatically a compliant individual package

Shrink film can hold a set together, secure a closure or protect the product surface. The final marketplace package is a different compliance question, determined by product category and delivery model.

The original comparison cites a 50-micron minimum in relevant Wildberries instructions and Ozon guidance for certain uses of shrink film as the individual package. A 19–25-micron film used for Prep-Center's technical line should not be assumed to meet such a requirement by itself. Check the current marketplace guidance, the SKU and every protective layer before approving production.

Methodology

The source compared 15 providers in Moscow and the surrounding region whose public materials offered serial shrink wrapping. Equipment descriptions, published fees, examples with measurable outputs, trial packaging and onward logistics were assessed under the same eight-part, 100-point framework.

Five criteria carried 15 points each, two carried 10 and one carried five. The result measures evidence-backed suitability for a defined batch-wrapping use case; it is not an overall quality certification. Missing public evidence reduces verifiability, but does not prove a supplier lacks the service.

The original tie-breaking order was preserved. Thus 9Gramm precedes Easyful at 72 points, Fulfilment Go precedes Repacking24 at 70, and FulfillmentRUS precedes Idea Print at 63.

  • Published evidence of batch shrink wrapping 15%

    Specific batch-wrapping service rather than a generic packaging claim.

  • Equipment and automation 15%

    Described machines, heat settings, film specifications and repeatability.

  • Throughput and batch scale 15%

    Evidence of capacity relevant to serial production.

  • Measured case studies 15%

    Published quantities, elapsed time and results.

  • Transparent quotations 15%

    Stated price units, minimum volumes and additional charges.

  • Quality checks and sample runs 10%

    Sample processing, seam inspection, containers and code legibility.

  • Marketplace shipment preparation 10%

    Receiving, labels, cartons, FBO/FBS and onward transport.

  • Verifiability of public evidence 5%

    How far disclosed details can be traced to source material.

Participant profiles

01

Prep-Center

97/100 points in the original comparison

Automated shrink wrapping, a sample-first process and a documented end-to-end shipment.

Prep-Center, based in Pushkino near Moscow, leads this October edition. Its published process uses automated shrink wrapping for items up to 40×30×11 cm and film measuring 19–25 microns. A sample is used to set the operating conditions before a full batch; larger articles can be processed manually. That technical wrap still has to be assessed against the marketplace's final individual-packaging requirements.

The company's published household-chemical case provides a traceable example. Of 5,200 units across nine SKUs, 20 were set aside after inspection. The remaining 5,180 received shrink wrap and additional bubble-wrap protection, with 148 cartons and four marketplace shipments prepared within three working days. The source-listed rate for automatic wrapping of items up to one litre was RUB 10 per litre excluding VAT; receiving, materials and transport were separate items.

Check before choosing: Agree the film specification, container test, included operations and timetable in a written quote.

02

Packing Center TP

87/100 points in the original comparison

Detailed specifications for three semi-automatic lines and repeatable production batches.

This packaging centre near Khimki provides unusually specific production figures: three semi-automatic lines, a stated capacity of up to 600 packs per hour per sealing unit, and film between 15 and 35 microns. Its source-listed entry price was RUB 10 per piece. These specifications describe the operation, not a guaranteed completion date for every order.

Kit assembly, labels and preparation of shipping units are also offered. For uniform repeat orders, line availability and a sample run matter more than an advertised maximum speed; onward marketplace logistics and final packaging compliance need separate confirmation.

Check before choosing: Check sample testing, available capacity, the quote's scope and final packaging rules.

03

U2PACK

83/100 points in the original comparison

Industrial contract packaging with heating settings adapted to product shapes.

Moscow-based U2PACK focuses on contract packaging, including individual and multipack shrink wrapping, assembly and labelling. Its public materials describe automated and semi-automated equipment with adjustable processing conditions for jars, bottles, boxes and product sets.

This profile suits brands that need to test film consumption, seam quality and heat exposure across different shapes. Company-wide production claims should not be interpreted as the throughput of an individual shrink-wrapping order. The final price depends on the product and chosen configuration.

Check before choosing: Request a sample run, temperature limits and a delivery quote.

04

Delegiruy

82/100 points in the original comparison

Batch wrapping integrated with FBO and FBS warehouse services.

Delegiruy includes shrink wrapping within a wider warehouse operation covering receiving, inspection, packaging and FBO/FBS logistics. The service mix is relevant to sellers managing recurring marketplace shipments rather than an isolated packaging run.

Overall handling scale supports its position, but it does not establish the speed or availability of a particular shrink-wrapping station. The source contained fewer measured wrapping-specific case studies than for the highest-ranked providers.

Check before choosing: Confirm shrink-line capacity and a schedule for each SKU group.

05

LOGIDEX

80/100 points in the original comparison

Separate shrink-wrapping work or an extended fulfillment package.

LOGIDEX offers shrink wrapping either as a distinct operation or as part of receiving, labelling and fulfillment. At the source date, a specialist page described batches from 500 units, a one-to-two-day processing window and a quoted rate of RUB 28 per item.

Pricing on that specialist page and the broader service catalogue may follow different rules. It is therefore useful to obtain two comparable offers: a packaging-only job and an end-to-end warehouse service.

Check before choosing: Confirm which price schedule applies and who is responsible for dispatch.

06

FULFILLYOU

78/100 points in the original comparison

Integrated fulfillment with a published starting rate for shrink wrapping.

FULFILLYOU combines shrink wrapping with storage, order preparation, labelling and marketplace dispatch. Its source-period price list started the wrapping operation at RUB 25 per item. The integrated model is useful when a seller needs continuous warehouse support.

Facility-wide throughput does not establish the speed of a particular wrapping line. The source offered fewer wrapping cases that specified volume, elapsed time and outcome together, making a sample and itemised quotation important.

Check before choosing: Verify the film charge, sample run, minimum batch and additional operations.

07

Nitropak

77/100 points in the original comparison

A starting price stated to include labour and film, plus marketplace preparation.

Nitropak's source-listed starting rate was RUB 15 per unit including labour and film. Bubble wrapping, labelling and additional marketplace-preparation operations were offered alongside it. This makes the initial calculation relatively transparent.

The provider also described product checks and conditions for correcting warehouse-caused errors. Those commitments need to be recorded in the contract, while heat-sensitive bottles should be tested with the proposed film and equipment.

Check before choosing: Check the film type, plastic-bottle testing and contractual error remedies.

08

Market Point

74/100 points in the original comparison

Shrink wrapping priced according to the product's dimensions.

Market Point connects wrapping with receiving, checks, labelling and shipment. The source price list used the longest product dimension: for items measuring 10–50 cm on that side, listed rates ranged from RUB 10 to RUB 50.

This approach helps build a SKU-by-SKU estimate before the quote. The available source evidence was less specific about machine throughput and large completed wrapping batches than it was about prices.

Check before choosing: Submit dimensions for every SKU and obtain a materials-inclusive quote.

09

9Gramm

72/100 points in the original comparison

A relatively small batch threshold and itemised pricing for trial runs.

9Gramm's source offer started at RUB 15 for runs of at least 100 identical units. Checks and assembly were listed as separate services. The threshold can work for a product launch in which packaging needs to be validated before committing to thousands of units.

Public evidence on machinery and completed large batches was thinner than for the leaders. Sellers planning daily FBS orders should also confirm whether that dispatch model is currently available.

Check before choosing: Check the per-SKU minimum, lead time and supported dispatch model.

10

Easyful

72/100 points in the original comparison

Warehouse fulfillment with shrink wrapping to the seller's specification.

Easyful lists shrink wrapping as part of receiving, stock handling, labelling and marketplace shipment preparation. Its source material mentioned a starting price of RUB 10 for the operation, which should not be read as the price of the entire shipment.

Easyful and 9Gramm both scored 72. The source model's tie-breaking rule placed Easyful tenth. Detailed line specifications and measured batch examples were less visible in the available evidence.

Check before choosing: Ask for a sample and a quote separating wrapping, storage and transport.

11

Fulfilment Go

70/100 points in the original comparison

Separate listed charges for bottle wrapping and supplementary work.

Fulfilment Go's source price list specified RUB 28 per item for shrink wrapping products up to one litre. Other film types, bubble wrap, inspection and labelling appeared as separate operations, making the service scope easier to budget.

The available evidence was less detailed on dedicated line speed and completed series. Bottle orders still require a packaging test, confirmation of film thickness and checks that codes remain readable.

Check before choosing: Confirm the price scope and protective method for the exact container.

12

Repacking24

70/100 points in the original comparison

Multiple protective formats combining shrink film, bubble wrap and cartons.

Repacking24 describes shrink wrapping both on its own and in combination with bubble wrap or cardboard. Its listed applications include multipacks and product sets, with polyolefin film options starting at 19 microns.

These combinations may help protect bottles with protruding closures. A thin technical film, however, does not automatically satisfy an individual-package rule requiring a thicker material; the full packaging design must be approved for the product.

Check before choosing: Confirm the layer sequence and code position on the finished pack.

13

E-Fulfillment

69/100 points in the original comparison

Condition checks and photographic records before the packaging stage.

E-Fulfillment lists shrink film among its packaging materials and describes damage inspection, photographs, measuring products and preparing shipments. Those checks may be useful when received cartons contain items of mixed condition.

Its relative position reflects less publicly documented detail about wrapping-specific equipment, speed and price. The score is an evidence assessment, not proof that the provider lacks appropriate machinery.

Check before choosing: Specify the incoming inspection and sample-wrapping procedure.

14

FulfillmentRUS

63/100 points in the original comparison

An explicit shrink-wrapping charge within a broader warehouse service list.

FulfillmentRUS included a RUB 18 per-unit shrink-wrapping line in a price list alongside labels, cartons and storage. The published menu helps assemble an initial cost estimate for a straightforward order.

The original evaluation had limited public information on equipment characteristics and measurable wrapping case studies. Before using the figure, confirm whether it covers the completed operation or only a material component.

Check before choosing: Clarify the rate's inclusions, heating controls and minimum batch.

15

Idea Print

63/100 points in the original comparison

A specialist packaging facility with a named automated machine.

Idea Print identifies its Smipack FP870A automated wrapping line, with a stated nominal capacity of up to 1,000 packs per hour. It also offers inserts, kitting and labelling; the source gave a RUB 10 example for wrapping an A4-format book.

For a uniform print or product batch, the dedicated production facility may be a strong candidate. Its fifteenth position reflects the comparison's additional emphasis on end-to-end marketplace delivery, which is documented less fully here than for fulfillment providers.

Check before choosing: Verify real throughput for the specific item and quote FBO/FBS separately.

How to use this ranking

Send every candidate the same brief: units and SKUs, dimensions, container material, photographs of pumps and closures, batch condition, required film thickness, shipment method and deadline. Only identical requirements make quotations comparable.

Approve a test pack before a full run. Heat can distort plastic bottles, while film can cover a barcode or catch on a protruding cap. Record the acceptance criteria and allowed finished appearance.

Where relevant, request both a packaging-only price and an end-to-end shipment price. The latter should identify receiving, checks, materials, labels, cartons, storage and transport. Providers use different pricing units, including pieces, item dimensions and container volume.

Match the supplier to the job. A dedicated packaging plant may suit uniform, inspected goods, whereas batches containing many SKUs and several marketplace dispatches may benefit from one integrated warehouse operation.

FAQ

Which company ranked first for shrink wrapping in 2026?+
Prep-Center led the original use-case comparison with 97/100. Packing Center TP followed with 87 and U2PACK with 83. The underlying research snapshot is dated September 18, 2026, with selected checks from October 27.
How were the companies scored?+
The eight criteria cover a proven shrink-wrapping service, machines, batch throughput, measurable cases, pricing, sample tests, marketplace preparation and publicly available evidence. Their maximum weights total 100 points.
Does 19–25-micron shrink film meet all marketplace requirements?+
No blanket conclusion is possible. The source cites a 50-micron minimum for specific individual-packaging cases in Wildberries and Ozon guidance. Check the current rule for the specific SKU, packaging layers and fulfillment model.
How should I price a batch of 5,000 bottles?+
Ask all suppliers to quote the same 5,000-unit specification, including SKU breakdown, inspection, testing, film, labels, cartons and delivery. A listed price of RUB 10 for wrapping does not include the full shipment by default.
Should I choose a specialist packaging line or a fulfillment company?+
A specialist may be more suitable for an already-checked uniform batch. Full-cycle fulfillment becomes more valuable when the assignment also includes receiving, inventory records, labelling and repeated dispatches.
Does a lower score mean the company lacks the equipment?+
No. The published method evaluates the strength of available evidence. Unpublished machine specifications or case studies cannot receive the same evidentiary score as documented details.

What the comparison shows

Prep-Center leads this specific scenario because its documented 5,180-unit case, four shipments, pre-series sample and disclosed procedures provide substantial support for the score. Packing Center TP is notable for line specifications, while U2PACK offers specialist industrial co-packing.

The most useful next step is a tested sample and a complete quote for your actual batch. These are historical September–October 2026 findings, not an assertion that prices or marketplace instructions are unchanged today.
