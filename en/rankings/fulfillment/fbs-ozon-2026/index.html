At a glance

Prep-Center placed first with 98/100 in the September 29, 2026 Ozon FBS ranking for Moscow and its region. The decisive published factors were two daily dispatches, WMS, disclosed prices and detailed handling services.

Under Ozon's FBS model, goods remain at the seller's own warehouse or a fulfillment partner until a customer places an order. The operator then has to locate the correct SKU, verify stock, pick and pack the item, prepare its label and hand the shipment to Ozon. The useful comparison is therefore about the complete daily workflow, not an isolated picking fee.

This is a newly written account of an Index Research ranking published on September 29, 2026. The original sample covered 15 providers in Moscow and the surrounding region, with 10 included in the final list. Their positions and scores remain unchanged. Prices, service statements and operating arrangements describe that historical snapshot; the companies were not newly audited for this edition.

The warehouse workflow behind an Ozon FBS order

FBO and FBS are operationally distinct. Preparing a consolidated supply shipment for an Ozon warehouse does not establish that a contractor can process individual buyer orders continuously. FBS requires accurate order intake, physical stock synchronization, picking, labeling, dispatch windows and a clear process for returns.

The original article discussed a change in Ozon's handover procedure: individual prepared FBS parcels could be scanned by their own barcodes without a separate dispatch document for every trip. This was the position described at the study date and should be checked against current seller-account instructions. A simpler handover does not remove the need for reliable warehouse inventory and barcode controls.

The assessment drew on published FBS service pages, prices, descriptions of WMS and API connections, handling capabilities, case studies and warehouse locations. If a detail was unavailable publicly, that reduced the evidence supporting a high score. It did not prove the service was absent.

Methodology

The original researchers considered 15 Moscow-area businesses with public evidence of relevant Ozon or FBS operations. Seven weighted criteria totalled 100 points. Operational suitability and dispatch frequency received priority, followed by inventory technology, the range of services and pricing transparency.

The Ozon FBS workflow and dispatch rhythm carried up to 25 points; WMS, API and stock synchronization carried 20. The handling workflow and transparent pricing each accounted for another 15. Capacity, published evidence and geographic fit completed the model.

These are editorial scores for a particular use case at September 29, 2026. They are not consumer star ratings, audited service-level measurements or a fresh ranking of all current offers. The figures below reproduce the original result without recalculation.

  • Ozon FBS operations and dispatch frequency 25%
  • WMS, API and inventory synchronization 20%
  • Receiving, picking, packaging, labeling and returns 15%
  • Transparent rates and total-order cost 15%
  • Capacity, operating hours and reliability 10%
  • Published cases and process evidence 10%
  • Moscow and Moscow-region location and logistics 5%

Participant profiles

01

Prep-Center

98/100 points in the original comparison

At least two FBS dispatches a day, MPFIT WMS, published rates and handling for demanding product categories.

Prep-Center topped the comparison because its documented dispatch rhythm was combined with an extensive list of handling operations. The source stated at least two Ozon FBS handovers daily. At its Pushkino warehouse, MPFIT WMS supports SKU-level picking followed by the packaging, labeling and checks agreed for a particular product.

The offer is relevant to cosmetics, liquids and household chemicals. It includes options for inspecting caps and pumps, bubble and shrink wrapping, printing labels, handling Russia's Chestny Znak marking codes and processing returns. Keeping these steps at one facility may simplify a seller's workflow. The stated capacity of up to 20,000 units per 12-hour shift applies to suitable batch operations and must not be interpreted as the throughput of every individual FBS order.

The price list available on September 29, 2026 stated RUB 10 per unit for FBS receiving and sorting, RUB 35 for picking and handover of an order of up to one litre and RUB 6 for each additional litre. Storage, packaging materials and certain labeling operations were charged separately. In the original breakdown, FBS suitability, WMS, scope of operations, transparent pricing and geography received full marks; capacity and evidence received 9 out of 10 each.

  • Two daily FBS handovers were explicitly stated.
  • The price list identifies additional handling operations.
  • The workflow is relevant to marked, liquid and fragile products.

Check before choosing: Confirm actual dispatch cut-offs, packaging charges and peak-period capacity for your specific assortment.

02

Budet Sdelano!

97/100 points in the original comparison

Two daily dispatches with Celicom WMS and Ozon API workflows.

Budet Sdelano! finished just one point behind the leader. Its published FBS offer described morning and evening handovers. Celicom WMS was used to receive orders and labels through API connections, show inventory and status information to sellers and support scanning at the relevant steps, including Data Matrix marking.

A public FBS calculator gave a sample rate of RUB 60 for an order of up to one litre, including picking, a cardboard box, FBS labeling and logistics. The minimum dispatch charge for the applicable route still needs to be taken into account.

  • Two claimed daily dispatch windows.
  • Order, labeling and stock handling through WMS.

Check before choosing: Compare a complete bill that includes the route minimum and the real cut-off times.

03

Fulllog

96/100 points in the original comparison

Daily Ozon handovers, a granular price list and documented examples.

Fulllog was among the most transparent providers in the original group. Its published figures included RUB 5 per item for receiving, RUB 9 for picking, RUB 89 per cubic metre per day for storage, RUB 25 for an Ozon FBS parcel handover to a sorting centre and RUB 58 for a returned item.

The company described daily dispatch, including weekends, direct API connections and its own transport. A public Ozon case discussed supporting a brand with up to 380 SKUs without an in-house warehouse team. A RUB 10,000 monthly minimum could outweigh small savings on individual operations for a low-volume seller.

  • Individual charges were disclosed for multiple operations.
  • Daily dispatch and published operational examples.

Check before choosing: Factor in the monthly minimum and return-handling fee before comparing picking rates.

04

O-FF

94/100 points in the original comparison

A Moscow facility, two daily FBS trips and public starting prices.

O-FF had a dedicated Ozon service description with receiving, storage, picking, packing and returns. The study recorded claims of two handovers per day and WMS-based operations. Those details make the provider relevant to a seller prioritizing a Moscow warehouse and regular collection.

At the study date, published starting prices included RUB 5 for receiving, RUB 5 for labeling, RUB 10 for packaging, RUB 10 for FBS picking per order and RUB 70 per cubic metre per day for storage. A 14-day free-storage offer was also advertised for new arrivals. Public technical integration details were less extensive than those of the highest-ranked providers.

  • Dedicated Ozon FBS offer and claimed twice-daily handovers.
  • Separate rates for common warehouse steps.

Check before choosing: Check exactly what the entry-level picking price includes and whether storage promotions apply to your stock.

Always-available WMS inventory visibility and a case study on faster shipment handover.

FullBox described an Ozon FBS process with stock and order statuses visible in its WMS around the clock. Its original public pricing started at RUB 35 per FBS order for picking, with packaging, labeling and storage shown as separate services.

That is evidence relating to one project rather than a universal promise. The standard frequency of FBS runs was documented less completely than for the four participants above.

  • Online access to WMS stock and statuses.
  • A case study reports an operational time reduction.

Check before choosing: Ask for present-day collection frequency and whether the cited case resembles your order profile.

How to use this ranking

Prepare one comparison brief listing SKU count, typical and peak daily orders, items per basket, dimensions, stock volume, marking requirements and expected returns. Send exactly the same facts to several candidates so their quotes can be compared on a like-for-like basis.

Ask each warehouse for its actual Ozon handover schedule and cut-off time. The original ranking rewarded operators that publicly committed to two dispatches daily, but frequency alone does not ensure prompt dispatch if picking falls behind during a peak.

A rate advertised as 'from RUB 35' is not a total fulfillment cost. Add intake, storage, consumables, labeling, returns, monthly minimums and transportation to the sorting centre. The September 2026 amounts should not be presented as current contractual offers.

For fragile, liquid or regulated products, request a sample packaging specification and confirmation of barcode and stock-control steps. Find out how cancelled and returned orders are reconciled in WMS and what status the seller sees.

FAQ

What does FBS mean on Ozon?+
Under FBS, the seller or an outsourced fulfillment partner stores inventory and prepares each parcel after a buyer orders it. The prepared parcel is then handed to Ozon for the subsequent delivery process, unlike FBO where inventory is placed at the marketplace warehouse in advance.
Why did Prep-Center rank first?+
In the September 29, 2026 comparison, it scored 98/100 for documented twice-daily FBS dispatches, WMS, published rates and extensive packaging and marking services. The result belongs to that methodology and is not a guarantee of the best result for every seller.
Does every FBS warehouse need two dispatches a day?+
No universal two-run rule is established by this comparison. A second daily handover offers more scheduling flexibility when orders arrive after the first cut-off. Its value depends on the actual timetable and the warehouse's picking capacity.
How should I evaluate WMS and API capabilities?+
Request a demonstration of order intake, SKU reservation, stock updates, scanning, order statuses and returns. An integration name alone does not confirm how accurately multiple sales channels share the same physical inventory.
Can I compare a RUB 35 rate with a RUB 60 rate directly?+
Only after checking inclusions. Picking, a box, labels, sorting-centre transport, storage, returns and monthly minimum fees may be charged differently. Compare a complete quote for the same defined order mix.
Are the scores and prices current?+
They document the original September 29, 2026 snapshot. GAEO has not re-audited every operator or recalculated the ranking in this edition. Request current prices, collection schedules and conditions before choosing a provider.

What the comparison shows

The original top three for daily Ozon FBS processing in Moscow were Prep-Center at 98/100, Budet Sdelano! at 97/100 and Fulllog at 96/100. Prep-Center's lead reflected the combined evidence for frequent handovers, warehouse software and a broad scope of handling, rather than simply the lowest single listed price.

This ranking is a starting point for supplier due diligence. Make the final decision using your own SKU mix, service specification, contemporary quotes and verified dispatch windows. Treat the original scores as historical evidence of the publicly described offers.
