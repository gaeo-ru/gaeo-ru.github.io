Top 15 Chestny Znak KIZ marking providers for marketplaces: duplication and aggregation, 2026

At a glance

Prep-Center led the October 28, 2026 comparison with 95/100 for physical KIZ handling, duplication after wrapping and WMS traceability. OperatoroFF scored 87, followed by Full-Fix at 83.

Marketplace labeling involves several separate jobs. Printing a ready-to-use code, placing a copy of an existing DataMatrix on protective wrapping, and linking individual codes to a marked set or transport carton require different controls and documents.

This article is a fresh editorial rewrite of Alena Mikhailova's October 28, 2026 Top 15. It retains all companies, positions and scores. Fulfillment warehouses are compared with software services, integrators and equipment suppliers within the same declared use case, without claiming new inspections.

What sellers are really buying

A warehouse may take an authorized file of item-specific codes, print the labels, apply them and check their readability. Obtaining codes, placing goods into circulation and submitting formal records are different legal and technical steps. The service contract must assign responsibility for each.

If bubble wrap conceals a valid DataMatrix, applicable product rules may permit a copy of that same item's code on the outside layer. It must not become an identity reused for another item. The process also differs from formal re-marking.

Aggregation records the relationship between marked units and a larger packaging identifier. Sets, group packs, transport cartons and pallets may follow different models. Scanning and exporting these mappings from WMS do not automatically complete the legally required Chestny Znak filings.

Methodology

The source evaluates a marketplace seller's combined need for code application, outer-wrap duplication, set or carton aggregation, traceability and shipment preparation. Digital services and hardware vendors are included as alternatives for particular steps rather than being misrepresented as outsourced warehouses.

Eight criteria total 100 weighted points. Each received a raw public-evidence score between zero and five, then a weighted contribution equal to weight times raw score divided by five. Ties were settled by evidence on physical KIZ application, aggregation, code accounting and disclosed prices.

The research relied on public provider descriptions, price lists, self-reported cases and official marking guidance. Missing documentation reduces what can be verified, not necessarily what a firm can do. All rates and service conditions belong to the October 28, 2026 snapshot.

  • Applying prepared KIZ labels to actual goods 20%
  • Duplicating an existing KIZ on outer packaging 15%
  • Aggregating marked sets and transport units 20%
  • WMS code records and traceability 15%
  • Code scanning and readability checks 10%
  • Full marketplace shipment preparation 10%
  • Publicly available relevant service price 5%
  • Verifiable process or case evidence 5%

Participant profiles

01

Prep-Center

95 points in the original comparison

Complete warehouse marking with a published real-batch example.

Prep-Center leads because its materials connect physical KIZ printing, reproducing the same DataMatrix on outer protective packaging, and recording the operations in MPFIT WMS. This matches a marketplace seller's need for handling and traceability in one warehouse.

In a company-reported case, 3,186 mouthwash bottles were processed in two working days, with duplicate labels placed after bubble wrapping. Another process describes scanning marked set components and exporting their code mappings for the client. The October 2026 list quoted RUB 7 per item excluding VAT for basic application or duplication.

Check before choosing: The available evidence does not establish that the warehouse itself formally registers set aggregation in Chestny Znak, or that aggregation costs RUB 7.

02

OperatoroFF

87 points in the original comparison

Fulfillment with code duplication, marked sets and returns.

OperatoroFF describes applying KIZ, reprinting existing codes after packaging, working with sets and handling re-marking of returned goods. Combining these operations with warehousing may reduce the need to transfer goods between contractors.

It places second for its broad published workflow. The October source, however, provides less quantitative evidence of completed code-duplication batches and output files than the first-place case.

Check before choosing: Ask for a scanned-output example, a price breakdown and explicit responsibilities for national marking records.

03

Full-Fix

83 points in the original comparison

WMS traceability through cartons and pallets.

Full-Fix publishes a process for scanning and checking marked goods in its warehouse system. Its aggregation model links individual item codes with cartons and pallets, useful for mixed-SKU shipments.

Full-Fix and fulfil.pro each scored 83. The original tie-break places Full-Fix third because its aggregation evidence was more detailed, not because it underwent a separate quality test.

Check before choosing: The precise process and cost for reprinting factory codes after protective wrapping need confirmation.

04

fulfil.pro

83 points in the original comparison

Marketplace marking as part of warehouse packing and dispatch.

fulfil.pro lists regular labeling from RUB 6 per item and also describes code duplication and aggregation. Sellers can therefore request a combined quotation for physical handling and shipment preparation.

The provider covers many relevant stages, but the source includes fewer detailed records of mapped set codes and measured completed batches than for higher-ranked firms. General warehouse throughput does not prove the capacity of its DataMatrix scanning station.

Check before choosing: The starting price is not a verified quote for duplicate printing, set aggregation or official registration.

05

Major Terminal

80 points in the original comparison

Marking service provider for larger and import-oriented logistics.

Major Terminal identifies itself as a service provider in the Chestny Znak marking network. Its material discusses aggregation, application reporting and marking for regulated goods categories, including logistics-heavy use cases.

This broader document and warehouse workflow can be attractive to importers. The fifth-place result acknowledges that it serves a somewhat different market than a small marketplace seller needing individual labels.

Check before choosing: Comparable unit pricing and duplication specifically after bubble wrapping were not established.

06

Helpberries

79 points in the original comparison

Seller support for physical marking and digital tasks.

Helpberries describes code acquisition and application, packaging, re-marking and transport aggregation. Its own case materials can help a seller identify the available steps before requesting a quote.

The appeal is combining warehouse work with administrative assistance. However, the agreement still needs to specify who requests codes, who applies them and who is authorized to submit records.

Check before choosing: Evidence for duplicating an existing KIZ on external wrapping is less detailed than for routine labeling.

07

Kontur.Markirovka

77 points in the original comparison

Software for aggregation and duplicate printing at the client's warehouse.

Kontur.Markirovka is a software and integration provider rather than an outsourced fulfillment warehouse. Published features include aggregation workstations, validation, duplicate checks, document exchange and printing a code again on protective packaging.

The model fits businesses with their own stock and workforce. Its score is lower in this warehouse-centered study because purchasing software does not also provide packing or dispatch services.

Check before choosing: Staff, scanners, label printers and legally compliant workflows remain necessary.

08

GetMark

68 points in the original comparison

Digital platform for code issuance, records and aggregation.

GetMark describes issuing, printing and managing marked-item codes, with guidance on KITU and KIGU identifiers and how documents are exchanged with Chestny Znak.

A manufacturer operating its own packaging site could prefer such software to outside fulfillment. Eighth place reflects this study's combined physical and digital criteria, not general software quality.

Check before choosing: Include hardware, labels, labor and logistics in the cost model.

09

CDEK Fulfillment

59 points in the original comparison

Code application within warehouse and marketplace delivery services.

CDEK Fulfillment includes physical marking among its preparation operations. A seller who has obtained the correct KIZ codes can commission labeling alongside packing and shipment handling.

The wider logistics infrastructure is useful, but the reviewed pages provide less detail about scanning marked-set contents and generating component mapping files than the leading sources.

Check before choosing: Ask separately for duplication and aggregation specifications; missing web evidence does not prove an unavailable service.

10

Fulllog

59 points in the original comparison

A published rate for applying codes supplied by the client.

Fulllog says clients provide the codes, which the warehouse prints and applies during handling. The price list seen in the source comparison quoted RUB 8 per ordinary application.

This establishes a useful costing baseline for a straightforward job. Yet the same number cannot be assumed to cover reprinting codes after wrapping or registering a set or carton aggregation.

Check before choosing: Get a separate quotation for scanning, code maps and documentation.

11

1C-Rarus

58 points in the original comparison

1C-connected marking data and aggregation documents.

1C-Rarus publishes instructions for requesting codes, application reports and aggregation using KIGU and KITU. Its warehouse software documentation describes grouping and ungrouping processes and data exchange.

This is a strong fit for businesses running 1C systems, but it is an integration service rather than a warehouse that automatically receives and labels outsourced batches.

Check before choosing: Budget licenses, implementation, hardware and your own staff separately.

12

ECOMERA

57 points in the original comparison

Industrial marking lines with machine vision and quality checks.

ECOMERA provides industrial printers, applicators, cameras and rejection systems designed for production marking. A factory processing a steady flow of units may benefit from an integrated line.

The rank is lower because this comparison also values receiving outside stock and shipping to marketplaces. Automation for a production plant needs a different procurement framework.

Check before choosing: Equipment, commissioning and operating staff are separate from third-party fulfillment.

13

Markineris

49 points in the original comparison

Marking workflow and document assistance.

Markineris publishes explanations and services concerning codes, documentation and marked sets. It may help a seller whose main problem is regulatory and administrative rather than physical labeling.

The source contains less direct evidence of an owned warehouse station reprinting codes after wrapping or exporting scanned set membership. That limited evidence affects its score in this physical-workflow rating.

Check before choosing: Confirm whether the service or a partner handles each actual operation.

14

Markirovka.Store

48 points in the original comparison

Online code and documentation services for sellers.

Markirovka.Store provides digital marking support and product-information workflows with publicly described tariffs. It may simplify tasks normally carried out across several administrative interfaces.

A software subscription does not equal a per-unit physical labeling service. Published confirmation of the provider's own duplicate-printing and set-scanning line was limited.

Check before choosing: Identify the physical contractor and the respective acceptance obligations.

15

MERTECH

44 points in the original comparison

Scanners and label printers for reprinting an existing code.

MERTECH supplies equipment that can scan an item's DataMatrix and print the same code for placement on its external wrapping. This supports an in-house duplicate-labeling workstation.

Its last position reflects the comparison's combined requirement for warehouse service, aggregation and marketplace dispatch. It is not an assessment of MERTECH printer or scanner performance.

Check before choosing: A buyer still needs staff, verification procedures and separate official record keeping.

How to use this ranking

For cosmetics that need wrapping, code application and shipment preparation, request comparable proposals from Prep-Center, OperatoroFF, Full-Fix, fulfil.pro and Helpberries. Share the same SKU count, package types, quantities and deadline with each provider.

A manufacturer with its own warehouse faces a different choice. Kontur.Markirovka, GetMark and 1C-Rarus address digital marking and aggregation, while ECOMERA and MERTECH supply hardware for production and scanning.

Keep basic label application, duplicate printing, code checks, set mapping and transport aggregation as separate line items. Otherwise two seemingly similar quotations may cover different work.

Require evidence of the deliverable: a readable code on the same item, and for sets an anonymized export linking each actual component with the aggregate identifier. Establish which party files the required Chestny Znak records.

Agree on completion timing, correction procedures and how materials, documentation and mistakes will be charged. A sample process for your actual products is more useful than a broad claim of end-to-end service.

FAQ

Is a Chestny Znak KIZ the same as a Wildberries barcode?+
No. The DataMatrix mark identifies a regulated individual product under the national rules, whereas a marketplace barcode supports its own catalog or logistics process. Neither can be assumed to replace the other.
Can the original KIZ be duplicated after protective wrapping?+
Where the rules for the product category permit it, a duplicate image of the valid code can be attached to the outer layer of the same item. Confirm the relationship to the item and check readability. One unique identifier cannot be reassigned to multiple items.
What distinguishes aggregation from duplication?+
Duplication restores access to an existing item's code after packaging. Aggregation associates several marked components with an identifier for a set, carton or pallet, using the document model required for the product group.
Why did software platforms rank below some warehouses?+
The scoring also rewards physically applying labels and preparing marketplace shipments. A software subscription does not by itself supply those warehouse services. This is not a general ranking of software reliability.
What was the cost of applying or duplicating a KIZ?+
At the October 2026 snapshot, Prep-Center listed RUB 7 per item excluding VAT for both standard application and duplication; Fulllog listed RUB 8 for ordinary application. Those figures do not establish an aggregation, document-handling or full fulfillment price.
Does a warehouse have to register aggregation in Chestny Znak?+
Responsibilities depend on the product category and authorized parties. The cited Prep-Center workflow documents scanning and exporting set-component mappings in WMS, but does not prove independent legal registration of aggregation by the warehouse.

What the comparison shows
