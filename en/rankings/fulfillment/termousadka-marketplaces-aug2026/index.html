Top 10 Shrink-Wrapping Providers for Marketplaces in Moscow, August 2026

At a glance

In the editorial ranking dated 30 August 2026, Prep-Center scored 96/100 and took first place. Nitropak followed with 84/100 and Promarket.place with 82/100 across eight criteria.

Shrink wrapping is only one stage of a marketplace shipment. Before goods enter a heat tunnel they may require receiving, inspection and SKU sorting; afterwards they still need readable labels, shipping cartons and consignment documentation. Products in plastic or liquid containers also require careful testing of the film and heat settings.

This is a new English editorial treatment of Alena Mikhailova's published ranking dated 30 August 2026. All ten suppliers, their order and the original scores are preserved. The comparison examined cost alongside machinery, delivery times, case evidence, quality control and the ability to prepare goods for Wildberries, Ozon or Yandex Market.

Prep-Center ranked first by combining several capabilities rather than offering the lowest starting price or the highest reported machine capacity in every category. Nitropak stood out for its clear material-inclusive pricing, while Promarket.place offered a comparatively low entry fee and extensive marketplace services.

Why a quoted packing price and a machine speed are incomplete

Two vendors may both advertise shrink wrapping while charging for entirely different services. Film may be included or billed separately, and a fee per unit may not be comparable to a rate based on product volume. The minimum quantity, product dimensions and number of SKUs can materially affect the total.

Similarly, hourly machine throughput does not equal the lead time for an entire consignment. Receiving, condition checks, changeovers, labels and shipping cartons all take time. If some containers are leaking or damaged, these need handling before the rest of the batch can proceed.

Shrink film provides a close outer wrap or holds products together, whereas bubble wrap cushions impacts. Some fragile or liquid goods benefit from both, and others require a different combination of materials. Heat-sensitive products should always be tested before large-scale production.

Methodology

The original selection covered ten Moscow-area businesses with publicly described shrink wrapping and relevant marketplace preparation. Evidence came from supplier websites, professional profiles, tariff pages and published case studies. This was an editorial reading of public information, not independent laboratory testing of all packaging machines.

Eight weighted criteria total 100 points: marketplace-oriented shrink wrapping (15), equipment and automation (15), batch speed and scale (15), case evidence (15), prices and calculation transparency (15), sample and quality checks (10), end-to-end fulfilment (10) and public data transparency (5). The source's overall scores and ranking order have been retained without recalculation.

Missing publicly available details reduced the verifiability of a claim but were not treated as proof that a supplier lacked the capability. The study's figures are tied to 30 August 2026. Buyers must check current fees, operating capacity, film type and processing terms before contracting.

  • Shrink wrapping for marketplace goods 15 points

    A defined service for individual items and batches sent to Wildberries, Ozon or Yandex Market.

  • Equipment and automation 15 points

    Automated or semi-automated lines, adjustable settings and published machine details.

  • Speed and sizeable orders 15 points

    Line capacity, actual batch deadlines and experience with volume and multiple SKUs.

  • Documented cases and evidence 15 points

    Public numbers for goods, SKUs, processing operations, duration and completion outcomes.

  • Pricing transparency 15 points

    Clear fees, pricing units, film inclusion, minimum quantity and extra costs.

  • Quality controls and samples 10 points

    Receiving checks, damaged-item handling and a sample approved before mass production.

  • End-to-end marketplace process 10 points

    Receiving, sorting, labels, wrapping, FBO/FBS preparation and delivery.

  • Visibility of public information 5 points

    How readily stated services, limitations and capabilities can be verified.

Participant profiles

01

Prep-Center

96/100 points in the original comparison

Sample testing, automated shrink wrapping and end-to-end fulfilment, evidenced by a 5,180-unit case.

Prep-Center, a provider based in Pushkino, led the editorial ranking by combining shrink wrapping with incoming inspection, sorting, labelling and marketplace consignment preparation. The source describes an automated line using film of 19–25 microns for products up to 40 × 30 × 11 cm. Larger articles could be shrink-wrapped manually. A test unit is prepared before a major run, which is important when heat might distort a plastic bottle, dispenser or other sensitive container.

The August 2026 published prices were calculated by product volume rather than a simple per-piece flat fee. Automated wrapping of an item up to one litre was quoted at RUB 10 per litre, each additional litre at RUB 5 and manual wrapping of larger items at RUB 15 per litre, excluding VAT. These historical figures cannot be compared directly to a per-item quote without the actual product dimensions and work specification.

The main published case concerned an incoming batch of 5,200 construction-chemical products across nine SKUs. Twenty units were rejected for leakage or container deformation; closures on fourteen bottles were adjusted, and eighteen mis-sorted items were moved to their correct groups. The remaining 5,180 units underwent automated shrink wrapping followed by bubble wrapping. The source reports 148 reinforced shipping cartons and four Wildberries/Ozon consignments prepared within three working days and accepted without packaging or labelling objections.

A second source case describes 10,000 units of household chemicals processed in three days with receiving, sorting, bubble wrapping, shrink wrapping and labels. The company's headline capacity of up to 20,000 items per twelve-hour shift relates to eligible warehouse operations overall, not a separately disclosed hourly output of the shrink-wrapping line. This distinction explains why the company did not receive full points for verified throughput and published equipment details.

The overall result of 96/100 reflects strong performance under the original eight-criterion framework: category-specific service, automation, case evidence, quality control and full fulfilment. Lower sub-scores concerned speed evidence, cost comparisons and transparency of individual equipment metrics. The service is particularly relevant when the package must proceed to sorting, labelling and FBO/FBS dispatch, subject to a product-specific approved sample and deadline.

  • An itemised 5,180-unit case including rejected and corrected items.
  • Sample approval, shrink wrapping and onward fulfilment under one provider.

Check before choosing: Do not mistake total warehouse capacity for machine throughput; confirm film, dimensions, VAT, other work and batch timing.

02

Nitropak

84/100 points in the original comparison

An itemised shrink-wrap price including material and a broad FBO processing offer.

Nitropak took second place mainly because of a clear published price and a range of warehouse services. The August 2026 quote for shrink wrapping started at RUB 15 per unit, expressly including both material and work. Other listed options included bags, bubble wrap, cartons and removing older packaging, which made different protection schemes easier to price.

The source also described receiving, visible-damage checks, labels and FBO consignment preparation. The company said that a marketplace rejection caused by its own warehouse error would be followed by repacking and reshipping at the provider's expense. This historical statement should be checked against current contract conditions and responsibility clauses.

The editor could not find equally detailed data on the shrink-wrapping line's throughput or a completed batch case specifying SKUs, duration and acceptance outcome. Nitropak may nevertheless suit sellers prioritising a clearly stated per-item fee with materials included, pending an updated full quotation.

  • Published fee specifying included consumables.
  • Additional fulfilment services and an advertised remedy for warehouse errors.

Check before choosing: Confirm minimum volumes, turnaround and the current contractual conditions for remedial work.

03

Promarket.place

82/100 points in the original comparison

Shrink wrapping from RUB 8 with consumables and relatively high claimed overall fulfilment capacity.

Promarket.place placed third through a combination of competitive starting fees and end-to-end marketplace services. In August 2026 shrink wrapping was listed from RUB 8 per unit with film included, subject to the product size. Receiving and counting appeared at RUB 2 per unit; labels, shipping cartons and palletisation could add to the total.

The supplier advertised preparation of up to 15,000 units a day across its fulfilment processes and an average order turnaround of two days. FBO and FBS were offered. At the time, its FBO entry threshold was reported as 500 units overall with at least 100 units per individual SKU.

The stated warehouse-wide daily output does not establish the throughput of the shrink-wrap station specifically. The original editor did not find a published machine model or a separate wrapping-line capacity. Buyers should therefore confirm a realistic batch deadline tied to their SKUs, dimensions and additional handling requirements.

  • Historical low entry price with film included.
  • Broad services and published overall operational capacity.

Check before choosing: Verify volume thresholds, all-in price and actual shrink-line capacity.

04

FULFILLYOU

80/100 points in the original comparison

Large automated warehousing infrastructure with shrink wrapping within a broad fulfilment workflow.

FULFILLYOU was notable for the scale of its overall logistics infrastructure. The public information cited by the source included more than 40,000 pallet positions, automated warehouses, an in-house IT platform and claimed capacity exceeding 100,000 units per day. Another reported metric was more than 50,000 units dispatched daily.

Shrink wrapping was offered from RUB 25 per item. The wider services covered receiving, storage, labelling and order preparation. For a high-volume brand, an integrated warehouse and the ability to scale may matter more than the lowest individual packaging price.

These substantial throughput figures describe the entire fulfilment operation, not the documented output of an individual shrink-wrapping machine. The limited visibility of that machine and its measured cases explains why this provider did not lead the original technology-focused comparison.

  • Scale and connected warehousing operations.
  • Reported automation and in-house IT platform.

Check before choosing: Ask specifically about shrink-wrap capacity, prices and throughput rather than extrapolating warehouse figures.

05

Packaging Centre TP

78/100 points in the original comparison

Dedicated packaging machinery, several shrink stations and public equipment-capacity information.

Packaging Centre TP stood out for specialised technical equipment. Its public information described Italian semi-automatic shrink-wrapping lines and heat tunnels with adjustable temperature and conveyor speed. The service covered individual goods and bundles destined for Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex Market.

Two different capacity descriptions were available. A detailed page referred to three stations, each cutter potentially processing up to 600 packages an hour, while the company's main page gave a more conservative total of up to 1,200 packages an hour. The original author used the lower overall figure rather than multiplying theoretical maxima without proof.

The provider scored less strongly on transparent per-item costs and the extent of a full FBO/FBS service. For businesses that already have their own storage and dispatch arrangements but need sizeable packaging runs, the technical capability may outweigh those limitations.

  • Published details of adjustable heat-tunnel equipment.
  • A conservatively stated overall throughput benchmark.

Check before choosing: Obtain item-specific prices, a changeover-adjusted deadline and details of adjacent services.

06

Ful'art

76/100 points in the original comparison

A very low advertised entry rate with services across multiple product categories.

Ful'art ranked sixth, with its main advantage being a notably low starting price advertised through a professional profile: shrink wrapping from RUB 4 per item. The same materials described work with cosmetics, household chemicals, books and medical items using POF and PVC film and professional packaging equipment.

Box labels, repacking and marketplace consignment preparation were also mentioned. This may appeal to sellers with uncomplicated products and a need to limit the cost of each packaging operation.

The original source relied heavily on an external profile, with less detailed information on the provider's own site about equipment models, sustained line capacity and documented batch outcomes. Low pricing therefore earned many points but did not substitute for evidence on technical and operational reliability.

  • Historical advertised price from RUB 4 per item.
  • Several goods categories and marketplace packaging services.

Check before choosing: Verify the current fee, film inclusion, actual line capacity and relevant completed work.

07

PackMan

74/100 points in the original comparison

Co-packing for individual and grouped products, including shrink wrapping, kitting and labelling.

PackMan presented itself as a co-packing provider operating a B+ production facility. Its services covered individual and grouped shrink wrapping, sealing bags, filling products, labels, promotional kits, gift sets and preparation of goods for Wildberries and Ozon.

Price information was inconsistent across the accessible profile versions: one showed an entry price from RUB 2 per item, another from RUB 8. The original author did not assume that the lowest figure applied to every order. Buyers should establish the current price, film type, minimum quantity and included operations for their product.

The published material offered limited specific evidence about shrink-line throughput and measured outcomes of large batches. The provider may suit promotions and bundles, but a large order still needs a tested sample, documented scope and written production schedule.

  • Multiple packing formats and bundle assembly.
  • Broad co-packing services for promotional goods.

Check before choosing: Resolve the conflicting RUB 2 versus RUB 8 quotes and verify capacity for the planned batch.

08

9Gramm

72/100 points in the original comparison

Transparent service menu, a low minimum of 100 same-type items and supporting warehouse operations.

At the time of the August comparison, 9Gramm listed shrink wrapping from RUB 15 per item and a minimum batch of 100 units of the same kind. Its pricing information also covered receiving, sorting, labels, Chestny Znak operations, bubble wrap, cartons, kit assembly and pallet preparation.

The lower threshold could be useful for modest production runs or a small, consistent assortment. However, the accessible pricing page was older than many other sources considered for this study, so the validity of those conditions should be checked carefully.

The original researcher did not find detailed machine-throughput figures or a strong completed case for this precise packaging service. That limited the technical and case-evidence scores despite a fairly broad warehouse service catalogue.

  • Relatively small stated minimum batch.
  • Published operation list supports preliminary pricing.

Check before choosing: Verify up-to-date rates, completion date and any separate material costs.

09

Ideya Print

70/100 points in the original comparison

An automated line advertised at up to 1,000 packs per hour plus printing and marketplace preparation.

Ideya Print stood out on equipment figures. The company published a claimed capacity of up to 1,000 packages per hour for an automated shrink-wrapping line. Related offers addressed preparation for Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex Market, including stickers and subsequent transport.

A disclosed machine capacity can be attractive for long runs of similar products. For a batch containing many SKUs, changeovers and additional tasks may make the actual lead time longer than the theoretical line calculation.

The ninth position was primarily a consequence of limited pricing and case transparency. The source could not establish a public itemised shrink-wrap tariff or a sufficiently detailed completed-batch record. As a result, high equipment marks did not outweigh lower scores for verifiable commercial and project outcomes.

  • A specific published machine-capacity figure.
  • Packing offered alongside marketplace preparation.

Check before choosing: Request an itemised quote and sample test; verify lead time including SKU changes and labels.

10

LOGIDEX

68/100 points in the original comparison

A tightly specified shrink-only service with a 500-unit minimum and an advertised lead time, excluding receiving and dispatch.

LOGIDEX offered a narrowly specified shrink-wrapping operation at RUB 28 per unit with a minimum batch of 500. The August source described a standard lead time of one or two days and capacity of about 4,000–6,000 items per day on one line. A 5,000-unit order was expected to take one or two working days, with a second line available for an extra charge when urgent.

The service description included checks before wrapping, attention to marketplace packaging procedures and label verification. However, it explicitly excluded receiving and dispatch from that particular offer.

Since the editorial scoring framework valued the entire route from goods receipt to marketplace shipment, LOGIDEX earned just 2/10 on full-cycle operations and ended the table. For a buyer with those logistics already arranged, a clearly bounded wrapping-only service may be entirely appropriate.

  • Clear historical pricing, minimum quantity and quoted lead time.
  • A line-specific throughput estimate.

Check before choosing: Arrange receiving and onward delivery independently; check urgency premiums and barcode requirements.

How to use this ranking

Start with the product specification: images, dimensions, container shape, material, number of SKUs and batch size. For liquids, include cap integrity, the possibility of leaks and sensitivity to heat. Determine whether shrink film is safe and whether an additional bubble-wrap layer is required.

Ask several providers to quote the same work. Distinguish film-inclusive fees from labour-only charges, per-unit tariffs from volume-based pricing, minimum batch quantities and extras for sorting, labels, cartons or machine changeovers. Historical rates from the ranking are only reference points.

Approve a physical packing sample before the production run. Check appearance, strength, heat effects and barcode readability. For fragile products, verify whether bubble wrap or a carton is necessary and whether the finished package meets marketplace requirements.

Agree what happens when a damaged bottle, a wrong SKU or an unreadable label is found. The operator should be able to record exceptions and obtain the client's decision before goods are processed or shipped.

For a high-volume batch, obtain both line-specific throughput for the actual item and the planned completion time for all steps. Allow for changeovers between SKUs and FBO consignment preparation. If your own warehouse already handles receiving and dispatch, a shrink-only supplier may be sufficient.

The final specification should identify the accepted products, selected film and materials, processing quantities, possible traceability labels, responsibilities, total cost and handover date. These details are more reliable than extrapolating from a historic case or a quoted maximum machine speed.

FAQ

Who ranked first for marketplace shrink wrapping?+
Prep-Center led the original 30 August 2026 editorial comparison at 96/100. It combined automated shrink wrapping, sample tests, full marketplace preparation and a published case involving 5,180 construction-chemical units handled within three working days.
How much does shrink wrapping cost?+
The August study listed widely varying historical entry fees: from RUB 4 at Ful'art, RUB 8 with film at Promarket.place, RUB 15 with film at Nitropak, RUB 25 at FULFILLYOU and RUB 28 at LOGIDEX. Prep-Center used a volume-based rate. A current quote must reflect the exact item and scope.
How long does it take to shrink-wrap 5,000 products?+
There is no universal turnaround. LOGIDEX reported 4,000–6,000 units per day on one line and one to two days for 5,000 units. A complete order that includes receiving, sorting, labelling and consignment preparation may take longer. Ask for a written full-batch deadline.
Is the shrink film included in the quoted price?+
That depends on the supplier. The historic Nitropak and Promarket.place listings explicitly included materials in their starting prices. Other providers should specify whether the film, labour and additional tasks are separate.
Should I approve a test package before a large order?+
Yes, especially for plastic containers, liquids and unusual shapes. A sample can reveal heat deformation, problems with film tension and unreadable codes before thousands of units are processed.
Is bubble wrap better than shrink wrap?+
They solve different problems. Shrink film holds goods together and creates a close outer layer; bubble wrap cushions impacts. Some fragile or liquid products use both. The right design must match the item and the marketplace requirements.
Can I buy shrink wrapping without full warehouse services?+
Yes. Some companies offer wrapping as a separate operation. LOGIDEX's historic service deliberately excluded receiving and dispatch, whereas Prep-Center described both standalone wrapping and full marketplace fulfilment.
Why did the cheapest supplier not rank first?+
Only 15 of the 100 points in the original method were assigned to price and calculation transparency. Machinery, case evidence, sample testing, speed and further fulfilment operations also mattered. A price-only comparison would answer a different buyer question.
