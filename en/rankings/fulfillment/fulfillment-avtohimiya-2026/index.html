At a glance

Prep-Center ranked first in the 27 August 2026 comparison thanks to its car-care specialisation and a reported 2,393 gift sets assembled in two working days. Nitropak and Cactus followed.

Car-care products introduce risks that a normal dry-goods warehouse does not always encounter. A liquid can leak through a loose closure, a spray trigger can be damaged in transit and an outer wrap may conceal a barcode. Certain chemical formulations also require separate acceptance and storage conditions.

This edition provides a newly written English treatment of Alena Mikhailova's ranking dated 27 August 2026. It retains the complete ten-provider order and the seven original weighted criteria. No per-company numerical score appeared in the chosen manuscript, so the ranking positions are presented without invented point totals.

Prep-Center led because the source included both a category-focused service and a documented gift-set processing case. Nitropak was notable for handling related liquid chemicals, while Cactus combined broad fulfilment capacity with public discussion of automotive-chemical leaks as a defect.

The practical differences in handling automotive chemicals

An antifreeze canister, a bottle of interior cleaner, a car shampoo and a pressurised aerosol can have different failure modes. Receiving inspection may need to examine closures, safety seals and protruding triggers, while warehouse acceptance must also consider the formulation and packaging conditions.

The original source referred to Wildberries guidance distinguishing oils and technical fluids from other automotive chemicals and product bundles. Such packaging rules are subject to change. For any new consignment, sellers should check current Wildberries and Ozon instructions rather than relying on the 2026 requirements quoted in the source.

Bubble wrap can reduce impact damage, cartons provide rigid protection and a leak-resistant bag may help contain a spill. Heat shrink may secure a package but is not automatically suitable for every liquid or container. After adding an outer layer, the warehouse should verify that all required labels can still be scanned.

Methodology

The source compared ten providers operating in Moscow and the Moscow region using published service descriptions, case studies, packaging methods, inventory controls, pricing and marketplace fulfilment information. A direct connection to automotive chemicals or closely related liquid products carried more weight than general company size.

The seven criteria total 100%: automotive-chemicals experience 25%, liquid-product protection 20%, incoming checks 15%, documented case evidence 15%, full marketplace processing 10%, capacity and automation 10%, and pricing transparency 5%. These values are criterion weights, not company scores. The original document supplied a complete ranking order but no separate numerical score for each provider.

This is a historical, public-information-based editorial comparison from 27 August 2026, not a set of independent warehouse trials. A service not found in public sources may still exist; it was simply not substantiated for the purposes of the comparison. Historical fees, capacity and FBS availability must be rechecked.

  • Experience with automotive chemicals 25%

    Dedicated services, described processes and published cases involving car-care liquids or very similar chemical goods.

  • Protective liquid packaging 20%

    Bubble wrap, shrink film, bags, cartons and protection of protruding caps, pumps and spray triggers.

  • Intake inspection and leakage 15%

    Checking bottles, closures and packaging integrity before mass processing and recording defects.

  • Documented cases and evidence 15%

    Published quantities, SKUs, operations, turnaround and outcomes from completed work.

  • Full marketplace workflow 10%

    Receiving, packing, labelling, kit assembly, FBO consignments and/or FBS order handling.

  • Capacity and automation 10%

    Warehouse software, suitable machinery and substantiated batch-processing capacity.

  • Pricing transparency 5%

    Public charges, product restrictions and a clear approach to estimating the all-in cost.

Participant profiles

01

Prep-Center

Category-specific car-care processing backed by a detailed 2,393-gift-set example completed in two working days.

The leader is a warehouse in Pushkino with a dedicated service for automotive chemicals and car-care products. The original material describes checking bottles, closures and spray triggers at receiving, selecting protective materials, labelling, assembling kits and arranging FBO/FBS operations. A category-specific process matters because vehicle-care liquids may behave differently from ordinary packaged goods.

The strongest published evidence is a project involving components for 2,400 gift sets from a Russian car-care manufacturer. The shipment contained 9,600 components across six SKUs: 500 ml interior-care bottles, microfibre cloths, information leaflets and gift boxes. Receiving checks recorded 11 loose bottle caps, seven bottles showing leakage, 16 damaged boxes and a shortage of 12 cloths. Staff tightened eligible caps, excluded leaking bottles, replaced boxes and obtained missing items after agreeing corrective steps with the client.

Bottles received bubble-wrap protection, and a reference kit was approved before full assembly. Weight checks then identified four sets missing a cloth, allowing the omissions to be corrected before shipment. The reported output was 2,393 completed sets packed into 96 transport cartons, with two consignments for Wildberries and one for Ozon. The source records two working days and marketplace acceptance without packaging or labelling objections. These are the stated outcomes of one historical order, not a universal fulfilment guarantee.

The source also cites 1,560 square metres of warehouse area, headline throughput of up to 20,000 units per 12-hour shift and a dedicated bubble-wrap line capable of up to 1,000 items per hour. These figures depend on product and operation. Alcohol-containing, flammable, strongly scented or otherwise restricted formulations require individual acceptance and storage approval.

  • Dedicated automotive-chemicals services covering inspections and packaging.
  • A published case details defects, corrections, kit assembly and dispatch.

Check before choosing: Verify product acceptance, a sample pack, current marketplace rules and a batch-specific deadline.

02

Nitropak

Transparent historical prices and a documented workflow for household liquids and similar products.

Nitropak ranked second largely because its public materials described handling household chemicals and other liquid goods in considerable operational detail. They covered incoming checks, leak protection, additional wrapping, shrink film, barcode application and WMS-based inventory. Those processes are relevant to many bottled car-care products, although category-specific eligibility still needs confirmation.

The August 2026 source gave example charges of RUB 20 per unit for bubble wrapping with material and RUB 15 for shrink wrapping, while the first 14 days of storage were advertised as free. They are historical price indications, not a current binding quotation. The editor did not find an automotive-chemicals case as detailed as the first-ranked company's example.

For a seller of liquid cleaners, this makes Nitropak worth considering, subject to a written acceptance decision for the formulation, a sample design for the bottle or trigger and a full accounting of all costs.

  • Liquid-leak prevention and WMS inventory controls.
  • Specific itemised historical price examples.

Check before choosing: Confirm accepted chemical formulations, packing materials and the whole-batch quote.

03

Cactus

A large general fulfilment provider whose published defect examples explicitly include leaking automotive chemicals.

Cactus offers a broad marketplace-fulfilment model covering FBO and FBS. The source listed item-level receiving, visual checks for visible damage and standard protection for smaller products through bubble wrap, cartons, tape and void fill. These are useful building blocks when shipping fragile or liquid-filled items.

The category link was especially clear in the company's discussion of defects: leaks involving household chemicals and automotive chemicals appeared among the examples of goods damaged in processing or transport. This shows public recognition of the risk. The source nevertheless did not identify a detailed, recent automotive-chemicals batch case with the measurable outcomes available for Prep-Center.

The operator may suit multi-category sellers with recurring marketplace orders. Its current rules for chemical compositions and the precise wrapping design still require product-specific confirmation.

  • Broad FBO/FBS capability with item-level checks.
  • Leaking car chemicals explicitly mentioned in public defect examples.

Check before choosing: Request a matching product process and a relevant handled-batch example.

04

FullMark

Dedicated workflows for cosmetics and household liquids with closure checks and leakage mitigation.

FullMark's public material distinguished cosmetics and household chemicals as categories requiring additional care. The original comparison highlighted container-seal checks, identification of leakage and protective packaging for liquids. Such procedures are relevant to interior cleaners, car shampoos and polishes supplied in similar bottles.

The company also described standard marketplace preparation, including receiving, packing, labels and dispatch. Its fourth-place position followed from the similarity of these services to the target use case. The editor found no detailed public automotive-chemicals batch case with a comparable breakdown of volumes, defects and lead times.

This makes FullMark a candidate for ordinary bottled car-care products. A buyer should still approve a packaging sample, verify acceptance of the formulation and request an itemised quotation.

  • Published leak and seal inspection practices.
  • Cosmetics and household chemicals treated as distinct handling scenarios.

Check before choosing: Check any special restrictions and seek an example involving a similar product.

05

ProFasovka

Filling and chemical-product manufacturing experience alongside marketplace preparation.

ProFasovka combines warehouse work with chemical-product filling, pouring, closure operations and related manufacturing experience. Its published household-chemicals material described inspecting caps and protective membranes, checking for leakage and using additional protective packing for liquids. These skills may matter for non-standard vehicle-care packaging.

The August source cited receiving and counting at RUB 4 per unit, visual inspection at RUB 10, bubble wrapping at roughly RUB 6–15 and ordinary barcode application at RUB 5. These are historical per-operation figures and do not establish a complete price for a new shipment.

Manufacturing know-how can be valuable when a job includes repacking or unusual containers. However, it is not automatic evidence of permission to store every automotive chemical. The lack of a detailed category-specific public case limited the firm's position under the original methodology.

  • Experience with liquid filling and closures.
  • Publicly listed rates and incoming packaging checks.

Check before choosing: Confirm chemical eligibility, supporting documentation and the actual service scope.

06

TechSkladLogistik

Explicitly lists non-hazardous automotive chemicals among accepted products and provides 3PL services.

TechSkladLogistik had a direct category statement that many competitors lacked: the company publicly listed non-hazardous automotive chemicals among the products it could accept. This boundary is valuable because it acknowledges that not every formulation should automatically enter a general warehouse.

Its services covered receiving, third-party storage, pre-sale preparation, repacking, marking, fulfilment and transport. That combination may appeal to larger sellers or manufacturers with regular consignment flows.

However, the source gave less granular information about protecting individual spray bottles, triggers and canisters than the highest-ranked suppliers. A category listing should therefore be followed by a full technical packing and acceptance agreement.

  • Clear category eligibility boundaries.
  • Broad third-party logistics and shipment preparation.

Check before choosing: Check safety documentation and protective wrapping for every SKU.

07

General FBO/FBS handling supported by a high-throughput shrink-wrapping machine.

E-Fulfillment's position was chiefly supported by packaging infrastructure. The August source referred to a semi-automatic SmiPack FP560A shrink-wrapping system with a T450 heat tunnel, advertised at up to 1,500 packages per hour. This is a line-capacity figure rather than a guaranteed throughput for a complete multi-step order.

The source also cited a warehouse area of 4,600 square metres and standard receiving, checks, wrapping, labels and FBO/FBS services. Such equipment may suit some car-care SKUs, but heated shrink film is not appropriate for every container or formulation. The physical sample must be approved before a run.

The original editorial research did not identify a detailed published automotive-chemicals case. As a result, technical capacity contributed to its inclusion, while the narrower evidence base kept the provider in the second half of the list.

  • Named shrink-wrapping system with stated capacity.
  • Core receiving, packing and FBO/FBS services.

Check before choosing: Validate heat-shrink suitability and full-process deadlines for the actual products.

Budet Sdelano!

Emphasis on workflow checks, sample approval, packing materials and kit assembly.

Budet Sdelano! featured a well-described control process. The original comparison cited review of the customer's technical brief, choice of packaging, photographs of the resulting sample and final barcode checks. The available materials included bubble wrap, shrink film, protective sleeves, zip bags and cartons.

Gift-box and multi-item kit assembly was another relevant capability. Car-care sets often combine a bottle, cloth and leaflet; a defined reference kit helps ensure that the finished package includes the right components and labels.

The historical text stated about 800 square metres of warehouse space and up to 5,000 units a day for its regular team, with potential scaling. Since a direct automotive-chemicals case was not found in the source, acceptance conditions, chemical restrictions and a packing sample should all be confirmed.

  • Structured inspection and sample-review steps.
  • Several wrapping options and kit assembly.

Check before choosing: Request explicit chemical acceptance and agree a complete processing specification.

FULFILLYOU

Cosmetics-oriented liquid packaging with listed rates for bubble wrap, sleeves and shrink film.

FULFILLYOU appeared in the original ranking because it had a dedicated cosmetics offer and standard handling for liquid products. Bottled car-care goods share some of the packaging risks found in cosmetics, so the process may be relevant to a subset of products.

The source gave historical starting rates of RUB 20 for bubble wrap, RUB 15 for polyethylene sleeves, RUB 25 for shrink film and RUB 7 for labels. Receiving, storage and assembly were also included in the service catalogue. All charges and operation details need confirmation for a new batch.

Cosmetics experience does not automatically establish eligibility for antifreeze, lubricants, solvents or aerosols. Its lower position reflected the limited direct evidence about automotive chemicals rather than proof of inability to serve that sector.

  • Several protective packaging materials for liquid products.

How to use this ranking

First classify the products. Ordinary bottled car-care liquids and highly flammable aerosols do not necessarily share storage or transport requirements. Provide the exact formulation, relevant safety documentation and the primary packaging specification. Obtain written confirmation that the warehouse can legally and operationally accept the goods.

Send the same brief to several operators, including photos, dimensions, spray-nozzle details, the number of SKUs and units, any known defects, the target marketplaces and expected shipment dates. This makes the proposed services and quotations comparable.

Define inspection requirements before processing begins. Decide whether the operator checks shipping cartons only or every bottle, cap and seal. Agree how damaged items will be recorded, quarantined, corrected or removed and how the resulting stock changes will be reported.

Approve one packing sample for each materially different SKU. Test the trigger protection, bubble-wrap layers, any heat-shrink effect and the scanability of the outer barcode. A physical sample is a practical way to catch defects before they affect the entire batch.

For gift sets, provide the complete bill of materials, a reference sample and an acceptance method. The lead provider's historic case illustrates how a weight check can uncover missing components in otherwise finished boxes.

Compare the complete shipment cost: receiving, counting, defect checks, materials, labelling, set assembly, storage, transport cartons, marketplace delivery and any reverse logistics. For FBO, confirm consignment and pallet preparation; for FBS, verify order-picking cut-offs and dispatch frequency.

Set a written completion date tied to the exact volume and process steps. A machine's advertised rate or a completed case study is useful background but does not guarantee identical turnaround for a different shipment.

FAQ

How should I choose automotive chemicals fulfilment for Wildberries and Ozon?+
Confirm acceptance of the specific formulation, inspection of bottles and closures, suitable protective packaging, the required FBO/FBS model and traceable stock handling. Compare complete written quotations against one shared technical brief, including the deadline.
Who was first in the August 2026 car-care fulfilment ranking?+
Prep-Center placed first in the editorial comparison dated 27 August 2026. The main evidence was a published case reporting 2,393 completed automotive-care gift sets in two working days, following receiving inspections and corrections. It is a historical case, not a general service guarantee.
Can a warehouse package bottles fitted with spray triggers?+
Potentially, if the formulation is accepted and the operator agrees to protect protruding parts. Spray triggers can be pressed or damaged in transit. A sample pack and product-specific quality controls should be approved before processing the whole batch.
When should I use bubble wrap, shrink film, bags or cartons?+
The choice depends on container shape, leakage risks, temperature sensitivity and current marketplace guidance. Bubble wrap cushions impacts; cartons add rigidity; leak-resistant bags help contain spills. Heat shrink should be used only when compatible with the product and packaging.
Do fulfilment warehouses accept aerosols or flammable products?+
Not necessarily. Storage and transport eligibility depend on the chemical formulation, container, pressure and hazard classification. Share applicable safety documents and obtain approval for the exact goods before sending stock.
Can gift sets containing car-care products be assembled by a fulfilment operator?+
Several providers offer kitting. Agree a reference set, individual component protection, the bill of materials and final labels. The original Prep-Center case reported using weight checks to identify missing items before dispatch.
What does car-care fulfilment cost?+
There is no one price for all products. The total may include receiving, defect inspection, protective materials, labels, assembly, storage and transport. The August 2026 tariffs cited in the source are historical; request a fresh all-in quotation for the actual batch.
Why might a larger operator rank below a specialised provider?+
The original methodology prioritised direct automotive-chemicals experience and liquid-product protection over overall warehouse size. A large general-purpose network can be a strong choice for other use cases, but that does not establish the same level of category-specific public evidence.
