Top 10 Household Chemicals Fulfilment Providers in Moscow and the Region, August 2026

At a glance

Prep-Center ranked first in the 24 August 2026 editorial comparison for its household-chemicals workflow and a reported 10,000-unit batch handled in three days.

A warehouse handling cleaning liquids or powders must ask questions that a standard boxed product may not raise. Bottles can leak at the cap, a trigger can break in transit and a powder pouch may split. Protective film may also cover the barcode, making a final scanning check necessary.

This article is a newly written English edition of Alena Mikhailova's ten-provider comparison dated 24 August 2026. It retains the original order and the seven weighted criteria used in the source. The source did not publish numerical scores for each company, so none have been introduced here.

Prep-Center led with category-focused operations and a quantified case. Nitropak followed for explicit liquid-products handling and visible prices, while ProFasovka stood out for combining warehousing with household-chemicals manufacturing and filling. The right supplier depends on whether the project requires standard FBO, daily FBS, production work or large pallet stocks.

Why cleaning products need their own warehouse brief

A trigger-spray window cleaner, powder detergent and gel in a canister present different risks. Incoming checks may need to cover seals, pumps, closure integrity, pouch seams and the possibility of leakage damaging neighbouring goods. One generic packaging specification is therefore insufficient for every household-chemicals SKU.

The original article referred to Wildberries packaging guidance updated on 14 August 2026, which differentiated protective materials by container and product type. For a new shipment, the marketplace rules and the product's own storage or transport requirements must be checked again. Aerosols, solvents and other specially regulated products may need separate acceptance decisions.

Marketplace shipping labels and Chestny Znak Data Matrix codes have different functions. Traceability obligations depend on the product category and current rules; wrapping may require an additional verification step to ensure codes remain readable. A warehouse's ability to apply labels does not by itself transfer every legal record-keeping obligation away from the owner.

Methodology

The original shortlist comprised operators serving Moscow and the Moscow region with public information about marketplace fulfilment and basic processing services. The comparison relied on service pages, product-category experience, published cases, packaging equipment, inventory controls and pricing. The geography and subject of the source have been retained.

Seven criteria make up 100%: household-chemicals experience 25%, leakage and container protection 20%, marketplace-compatible wrapping 15%, FBO/FBS operations 15%, inventory control 10%, capacity and turnaround 10%, and transparent prices plus evidence 5%. These are weights, not the companies' individual scores. The original article listed the ranking order but did not publish a separate numerical total per provider.

This is a historical editorial assessment, not a new warehouse audit. Missing public evidence was treated as a limit on what could be confirmed, not proof that a supplier lacked a service. Historic prices and marketplace conditions need fresh verification before use.

  • Household-chemicals expertise 25%

    Published category-specific services, products and cases involving liquids and powders.

  • Container integrity and leakage control 20%

    Inspection of caps, triggers and seals and measures to protect liquids and powders.

  • Marketplace-compatible packaging 15%

    Bubble wrap, shrink wrap, bags, cartons and readable exterior labels.

  • End-to-end FBO/FBS 15%

    Receiving, sorting, packing, marking, consignment dispatch and/or daily order processing.

  • Inventory and process controls 10%

    WMS or other stock records, SKU visibility and scanning procedures.

  • Capacity and turnaround 10%

    Published throughput, equipment and quantified order processing times.

  • Pricing and evidence 5%

    Itemised charges, public cases and verifiable operational information.

Participant profiles

01

Prep-Center

Product-specific inspection and protective wrapping supported by a published 10,000-unit batch case.

Prep-Center, based in Pushkino, took first place because the original material describes a workflow tailored to household chemicals rather than only general fulfilment services. The product range includes liquid cleaners, detergents, powders, granules, canisters and containers with triggers or pumps. Receiving, SKU sorting, container inspection, protection, labelling and shipment creation are arranged according to the customer's specifications. Packaging may combine bubble wrap, bags, cartons and shrink wrap as appropriate to the item.

The strongest supporting evidence in the 24 August 2026 source is a reported 10,000-unit project completed in three days. The described work comprised receiving, sorting, bubble wrapping, shrink wrapping and applying standard labels and QR codes. Its total reported cost was RUB 180,000, or RUB 18 per item for that particular volume and set of services. This is a historical project cost, not a current universal price.

At the source date, the company reported 1,560 square metres of warehouse area and total potential processing of up to 20,000 units per 12-hour shift, subject to operations. An automated bubble-wrap line was described as capable of up to 1,000 items per hour; shrink-wrapping equipment was also listed. The source explicitly warns that the film used by one machine does not automatically meet every marketplace packaging specification for liquids. A product-specific sample and written packaging brief remain necessary.

The offer covers both FBO consignment preparation and FBS storage with individual order processing. This combination matters if a seller uses both fulfilment models. However, the operator should confirm the exact chemical composition, packaging requirements and acceptance conditions before receiving aerosols, solvents or other products subject to special restrictions.

  • Category-specific service and a documented historical batch with quantity and lead time.
  • Bubble-wrap and shrink-wrap processes plus FBO/FBS workflows.

Check before choosing: Verify product eligibility, a packing sample, current marketplace requirements, itemised fees and lead time.

02

Nitropak

Dedicated liquid and household-goods processing, WMS and a published tariff structure.

Nitropak ranked second because its public service description expressly covered household products and liquids. The original August text referred to a warehouse in Bittsa of around 1,000 square metres, proprietary WMS software and end-to-end FBO preparation for Wildberries and Ozon. Packaging inspection, shrink wrapping, leakage protection and applicable Chestny Znak labelling were part of the listed services.

The original material also cited visible starting fees: certain combined operations from RUB 2 per item, delivery from RUB 1,400 per pallet and potentially free storage for the first 14 days. Those figures reflected published August conditions and are not comparable to an all-inclusive shipment quote. A seller should obtain a fresh, itemised price covering checks, materials, storage and shipping.

  • Explicit information on liquid household products.
  • Warehouse software and visible starting rates.

Check before choosing: Confirm the scope of advertised minimum rates and the packing design for the actual container.

03

ProFasovka

Fulfilment combined with contract manufacturing and filling of household-chemical products.

ProFasovka was placed third because the company combined fulfilment with contract manufacturing and filling of household chemicals. The source described experience with liquids, powders and granulated products, including inspecting primary packaging, adding protective materials and preparing goods for marketplaces. That is relevant where the assignment goes beyond ordinary barcoding and boxing.

The model can suit brands needing filling, repacking, promotional kits or production-related handling alongside distribution. However, any claims regarding aggressive chemicals or dangerous goods must be checked against the exact formulation, documentation and storage rules. Manufacturing experience alone does not establish permission to accept every chemical product.

  • Product-category manufacturing expertise.
  • Potential fit for filling and specialised packaging.

Check before choosing: Verify eligible formulations, transport conditions and responsibility for non-standard work.

04

Contract Factory

Household-chemicals contract production paired with packing, storage and marketplace shipments.

Contract Factory's position reflects experience with manufacturing liquid and powder household chemicals alongside warehouse services. The source listed receiving, counting, agreed package inspection, bubble wrapping for liquids, shrink wrapping, individual microcorrugated cartons and labelling. Shipments to Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex Market were among the stated options.

The original account placed its facilities near Khimki and cited capacity for more than 600 Euro pallets. This may fit brands with production-linked batch shipments. A seller requiring daily item-level FBS dispatch should verify that separately: the source provided more concrete detail about preparing and sending consignments than processing individual consumer orders.

  • Manufacturing experience with liquids and powders.
  • Several protective packing methods and batch dispatch.

Check before choosing: Request confirmation of daily item-level FBS processing if required.

logixPRO

Large-scale 3PL warehousing suited to palletised household-chemicals flows.

logixPRO was ranked in the middle despite infrastructure much larger than that of many other suppliers. The source identified class A facilities in the Moscow region, household-chemicals storage, environmental controls and security monitoring. Manhattan SCALE WMS was presented as the system for location-based inventory tracking and stock movements.

The original article reported combined capacity exceeding 90,000 pallet positions as well as item-, carton- and pallet-level picking, labelling, inventory checks and transport. This can be attractive to manufacturers and importers with major inventories. Large-scale 3PL capabilities, however, do not prove favourable terms for a small seller's daily FBS orders. Those workflows need their own quotation.

  • Large pallet capacity and location-based WMS.
  • Dedicated household-chemicals storage information.

Check before choosing: Confirm minimum volumes, project costs and small-order fulfilment arrangements.

The lower position reflected relatively limited public detail about handling household chemicals specifically. A broad service portfolio may suit a varied assortment, but liquids and powders require separate confirmation of product eligibility, inspection and packing materials.   • Itemised wrapping and inspection rates.

Pre-approved sample packs and multi-stage barcode verification.

Handee's distinctive feature was a pre-production approval process. After receiving and location-based storage registration, one example of each SKU would be packed and filmed for the client to approve. The source also discussed scanning every item, verifying label readability, four stages of label checking and FBO/FBS services. Category-specific evidence for household chemicals was less extensive. Sellers of liquids and granules should confirm accepted formulations and packaging methods before committing a batch.

  • Packing-sample approval before full-scale production.
  • Multiple barcode-verification steps.

How to use this ranking

Provide three to five prospective warehouses with the same product pictures, container dimensions, cap and trigger details, quantities and SKUs, relevant chemical properties, temperature requirements and target marketplace. This makes eligibility and processing proposals comparable.

Define the receiving inspection precisely: which defects will be photographed, what will be separated and who decides whether damaged stock is rejected, repacked or returned. For powders, pouch seams and moisture protection matter; for liquids, caps and pumps need specific attention.

Approve a physical packing sample before processing thousands of units. Check whether heat shrink affects the container, whether bubble wrap provides enough protection and whether all required codes remain readable after the outer layer is applied.

Compare itemised quotations for receiving, sorting, checks, bubble wrap, shrink wrap, bags, cartons, labels, storage, returns and transport. A historical starting price per operation is not the same as a complete current quote for the job.

For FBO, confirm carton and pallet creation, shipping labels and the distribution-centre delivery schedule. For FBS, ask about daily cut-off times, picking of individual orders, dispatch frequency and synchronisation of inventory. Their economics and operational constraints differ.

Set a written deadline linked to a precise batch volume and process specification. Equipment throughput and a historic project time are not unconditional guarantees for another order.

FAQ

How should I choose a household-chemicals fulfilment provider?+
First confirm acceptance of the exact formulation and container type. Then compare intake inspection, protective packaging, inventory visibility, FBO/FBS capabilities, timing and total cost against one shared specification. A detailed completed-case report is more meaningful than an unsupported speed claim.
How can a warehouse reduce the risk of leaking bottles?+
Agree how caps, pumps and triggers will be inspected, then choose packaging matched to the container. Bags, bubble wrap and cartons may be used in combination. External protection reduces damage risk but cannot guarantee that a defective bottle will never leak.
Should I use bubble wrap or shrink wrap?+
Bubble wrap provides impact protection; shrink wrap holds an outer package together. Some products need both, while others require a different solution. Marketplace rules, the bottle's geometry and its tolerance to heat should guide the final sample.
Does all household chemistry require Chestny Znak labels?+
No. The obligation depends on the exact product classification and current regulations. For eligible products, agree who supplies, prints, applies and checks the Data Matrix codes and who handles the associated reporting. A marketplace shipping barcode is not a substitute.
What did household-chemicals fulfilment cost in the source?+
Prep-Center's published historical example reported RUB 180,000 for 10,000 units, equivalent to RUB 18 per item for the stated set of operations in 2026. It is not a current standard rate. A new quotation must account for the product, checks, materials, marking, storage and delivery.
Can powders and liquids be kept in the same facility?+
They can be accepted in one facility when its storage conditions and policies suit each specific product. Powder packaging must be protected from tears and moisture; liquids need closure checks and transport protection. Confirm compatibility and any segregation requirements in advance.
What is the practical difference between FBO and FBS?+
Under FBO, the warehouse prepares a bulk consignment for a marketplace distribution centre, including shipping cartons or pallets. Under FBS, the provider stores goods and dispatches individual consumer orders repeatedly. The timing and service charges can differ substantially.
Why approve a packing sample before a large batch?+
A sample allows the buyer to test cap protection, wrapping quality, code readability and the effects of shrink-wrapping heat before the method is repeated thousands of times. It can prevent costly large-scale rework.
