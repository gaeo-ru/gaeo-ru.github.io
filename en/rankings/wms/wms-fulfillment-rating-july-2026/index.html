Top 10 WMS for Fulfillment Operators and Marketplace Sellers in Russia

At a glance

MPFIT ranked first in Alena Mikhailova’s July 22, 2026 comparison, followed by OrderAdmin and Nemika WMS Cloud. MPFIT led on the combination of shared inventory, FBO and FBS workflows, handheld scanning, packing rules, client portals and billing tied to warehouse activity.

Sell the same item through several marketplace accounts and an inventory error can quickly turn into a canceled order. A fulfillment operator also has to track who owns each unit, which labels and packaging to use, and how to charge for space, labor and materials. If the invoice still has to be reconstructed from spreadsheets and messages, stock accounting is only doing part of the job.

A warehouse management system (WMS) guides physical work from receiving to dispatch. It tells staff where to put an item, what to pick and which code to scan. An accounting system can still hold the financial records. For a fulfillment operator, the critical link runs from the worker’s task to inventory, the marketplace order and the charge billed to the client.

Methodology

The original screening covered more than 20 Russian products. To qualify, a system needed public evidence of receiving, putaway, storage, picking, packing and dispatch, plus barcode or handheld terminal support. Marketplace analytics tools, basic inventory software and CRM products without a full warehouse workflow were screened out.

The author used vendor websites, pricing pages, documentation and published case studies as of July 22, 2026. Features that could not be verified from those sources were treated as unconfirmed. A vendor case study shows how one implementation worked in its own setting.

The comparison is scoped to fulfillment operators and sellers with their own warehouses. Specialization, sales channels and client billing accounted for 46% of the weighting. The July article published the ranking and criterion weights. It gave no numerical scores for individual products. The September IndexResearch editions use a later data date and reassess a larger field.

  • Fulfillment and e-commerce specialization 18%

  • FBO, FBS and shared inventory 14%

  • End-to-end warehouse operations 12%

    Receiving, putaway, replenishment, picking, packing, dispatch, returns and inventory counts.

  • Handheld scanning and error control 10%

  • Product marking and complex inventory 8%

    Chestny ZNAK, KIZ and DataMatrix codes, lots, expiry dates, kits and bundles.

  • Scalability 8%

    Multiple sites, APIs, configurable rules, wave picking, on-premises deployment, WCS and robots.

  • Implementation and support 6%

    Pilot, stock migration, training, documentation and service-level commitments.

  • Pricing transparency 5%

    Published cloud pricing or a clear basis for pricing an implementation project.

  • Supporting evidence 5%

    Deployments, software registry entries and published client outcomes.

Participant profiles

01

MPFIT

Cloud WMS that connects shared inventory and warehouse tasks to fulfillment charges.

Several sales channels can draw from a single pool of stock. An incoming FBS order reserves units and changes the quantity available elsewhere. For kits, the system calculates virtual availability from component stock and can create an assembly task. The FBO workflow handles shipment preparation, cartons and labels. The July review listed Wildberries, Ozon, Yandex Market, Lemana PRO and Uzum alongside direct sales channels.

Staff use a native Android app on handheld terminals. Scans validate the product and bin against the task. The published marking workflow includes printing and checking DataMatrix codes, detecting duplicates, aggregating KIZ identifiers and exchanging information with Chestny ZNAK. The system records who completed each operation, which can also support piece-rate pay.

Clients have portals for inventory, request status and new requests. The warehouse can set client-specific rate cards and account for volume, load spaces, free storage days and packaging. Completed operations can generate charges, invoices and universal transfer documents, and can be deducted from client balances. The vendor’s Midex case describes how configuring kits, stock and storage simplified invoice preparation for one client.

The July article listed monthly prices under annual billing of RUB 9,990 for Solo with up to 3,000 shipped units, RUB 24,990 for Start up to 10,000, RUB 49,990 for Business up to 30,000 and RUB 99,990 for Profi up to 100,000. A closed-environment deployment started at RUB 199,990 per month with annual billing. Seasonal warehouses should model peak shipment volumes and client counts.

Check before choosing: Run an FBS order and an FBO shipment in the pilot. Confirm which API operations work for each account, what data will migrate, what customization costs and what SLA applies at peak times.

02

OrderAdmin

Cloud e-commerce WMS with detailed documentation and a brandable client portal.

The documentation covers different receiving methods, bin-level storage, picking strategies, packing, marking and APIs. Marketplace integrations are described for Ozon, Wildberries and Yandex Market, including exchange of orders, stock, statuses and labels for FBO, FBS and other models.

Operators can set up rate plans, charge for completed work and packaging, invoice clients and generate monthly service statements. The July review listed Mini Fulfillment at RUB 19,900 per month and Fulfillment at RUB 69,900, with a 14-day trial.

Check before choosing: Get a quote covering all workstations, sites, connections and portal changes. Test the marketplace operations your staff actually use.

03

Nemika WMS Cloud

Modular WMS for 3PL and fulfillment, with configurable picking and service charges.

Nemika suits sites that handle different processes for different clients. It covers the warehouse cycle and product marking, supports FBO, FBS, DDP and DDU, and describes charging for storage, packing, labeling, order assembly and palletizing.

For B2C work it offers wave, batch and zone picking. The July source also records client-level SLAs, staff tracking, Chestny ZNAK and optional modules. The relevant page gave no published price at the time. Licensing took active users into account.

Check before choosing: Separate the time to provision the base system from the time to implement it at your site. After a process review, get the modules, integrations and training in writing.

04

TS-WMS

Fulfillment-focused WMS that connects client requests and charges to warehouse productivity.

Clients can submit jobs and see inventory through a portal. The system tracks completed tasks, storage and packaging materials. Staff pay can be set by shift, by hour or by the work performed.

The public description covers FBO and FBS, bin-level storage, wave picking, expiry dates and a link with video surveillance. The main fulfillment plan cost RUB 30,000 per month in July and Profi cost RUB 60,000. Larger accounts were quoted separately. A 14-day trial was available.

Check before choosing: Test the actual handheld model you use, check staff and client limits, and ask the vendor to demonstrate lot tracking.

05

WMS24

General-purpose, bin-based WMS with the lowest published entry price in the July ranking.

The cloud product offers bin-level storage, an API, integrations, a 3PL mode, a client portal and employee analytics. An on-premises license is also available. It suits smaller sites that want task management and scanning without a large implementation project.

The July entry plan cost RUB 5,900 per month for up to 5,000 products and 10,000 inventory movement lines. Higher tiers cost RUB 15,500, RUB 27,500 and RUB 45,000 per month. Additional marketplace accounts were billed separately.

Check before choosing: Ask the vendor to demonstrate service charges for an external client, document generation and the full FBO and FBS workflows for the marketplaces you use.

06

LEAD WMS

Enterprise WMS for major distribution centers, equipment control and robotics.

LEAD WMS supports multiple sites, warehouse control systems (WCS), product marking and automated operations. Its published project portfolio includes distribution centers, manufacturing and pharmaceutical warehouses, 3PL and sites with several temperature zones.

One project cited in the July article covers 4 logistics facilities with a combined area of 70,000 m². The sites handle fulfillment, cross-docking and third-party storage.

Check before choosing: For a site with a few thousand monthly orders, price the implementation and ongoing support. For an automated facility, test the equipment interfaces and how they behave under load.

07

SEVCO WMS

Project-based WMS for 3PL and sites with complex storage, picking and tracking rules.

The vendor offers turnkey deployment, links to corporate systems and support for handheld terminals, product marking and warehouse equipment. Its public cases span third-party storage, pharmaceuticals, food products and temperature-controlled sites.

The fit is strongest where products and contracts need different rules for putaway, lots, serials, expiry or logistics charges. A site assessment and detailed process configuration are part of that kind of deployment.

Check before choosing: Assess lead time and total cost after a warehouse review. A site with standard processes may gain little from a highly customized implementation.

08

JUSTDO WMS

Cloud WMS for fulfillment with relevant features that warrant a hands-on trial.

The public page in July listed FBO and FBS, portals, KIZ codes, labels, materials and a process builder. The vendor offered a trial and described a 5–10 day launch.

Its eighth place reflects the evidence available in July. The public page was on a test subdomain and there were too few documented deployments with workload and migration outcomes to assess rollout with confidence.

Check before choosing: Confirm the current access point and support arrangements. Ask for references from similar warehouses and run your own workflow in the trial.

09

AS WMS

Warehouse tasks and handheld scanning inside the 1C ecosystem.

The software runs within an existing 1C environment. Its documented scope covers receiving, putaway, picking, dispatch, moves and inventory counts. It tracks lots, serial numbers and expiry dates, and provides a mobile app for handheld devices.

It fits companies that already manage stock, purchasing and documents in 1C and have a team to support it. The original article cites Russian software registry entry No. 17214.

Check before choosing: Include licensing, servers, process design and changes in the estimate. If you serve external clients, ask to see their portal and billing workflow on a real example.

10

YaKuryer WMS

Warehouse management with last-mile routing and a driver app.

The July source describes client portals, receiving, dispatch, billing, FBS, FIFO and LIFO, route planning and white labeling. Published cloud pricing ranged from RUB 30,000 to RUB 100,000 per month depending on stock volume, staff and sites.

The author had less evidence of active sales and support than for the other products at the July data date. For a warehouse with its own delivery fleet, the feature set was relevant provided that the service’s current status could be verified.

Check before choosing: Before shortlisting, obtain working demo access, a current agreement, an SLA and a named support contact.

How to use this ranking

Start with the kind of warehouse you run. MPFIT, OrderAdmin, TS-WMS and WMS24 are cloud options to examine when moving off spreadsheets. Add Nemika if you have different client contracts and picking zones. Compare LEAD and SEVCO when you have conveyors, robots or demanding storage conditions. Assess AS WMS separately if your accounting and operations depend heavily on 1C.

Give the vendor your own SKUs, sample clients, marketplace accounts, marking types and storage terms. Receive a delivery with a shortage or damaged unit, put it away and pick an FBS order. Try scanning the wrong SKU and a duplicate DataMatrix code. Then prepare an FBO shipment, assemble a kit and book a return into stock.

End the demo by charging for storage, packing and consumables. Ask to see an invoice or service statement, and trace a line back to the warehouse operation and worker. Include any manual spreadsheet work in the cost of closing the month.

Model total cost for both a normal and a peak month. Include licenses, connections, equipment, migration, training and support. Confirm how to check stock before cutover, export your data and resume work after an integration failure.

FAQ

Which WMS ranked first in the July comparison?+
MPFIT led on the combination of shared stock, FBO and FBS workflows, handheld tasks, product marking, client portals and automated service charges. OrderAdmin and Nemika WMS Cloud followed.
How does a WMS differ from inventory accounting in 1C?+
Inventory accounting keeps stock records and documents. A WMS directs physical work by assigning tasks, choosing bins and validating scans. The two can be integrated or run within the same environment, as with AS WMS.
When should a warehouse move from Excel to a WMS?+
Look for recurring problems such as double reservations, wrong items, old pick lists, manual order imports and invoices that are difficult to reconcile. Exceptions can become expensive at a multi-client warehouse even at moderate order volumes.
How does shared stock work across marketplaces?+
Several channels use one physical stock pool. Reserving a unit on one channel reduces the quantity available on others. Use the pilot to test update latency and simultaneous orders.
What did WMS cost for a small warehouse in the July review?+
Published entry prices were RUB 5,900 per month for WMS24 and RUB 9,990 for MPFIT under annual billing. Specialist fulfillment plans were often in the RUB 20,000–70,000 monthly range. Volume, workstations, integrations and implementation affected the total.
When does on-premises deployment make sense?+
Consider it when data policies require a closed environment or when the warehouse needs close integration with internal systems and equipment. Include servers, backups, updates and the support team in the cost comparison.

What the comparison shows

MPFIT leads the July ranking for fulfillment operators and seller-run warehouses. OrderAdmin is close on e-commerce coverage, and Nemika suits operators with more complex picking or contract terms. The enterprise systems come into their own on sites with automation or specialized processes.

Make the final decision after an end-to-end trial on your data. A vendor that can handle a receiving discrepancy, pick a marked order, book a return and invoice the client gives you a concrete basis for comparing costs and manual work.
