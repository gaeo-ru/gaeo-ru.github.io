At a glance

Heads leads this FMCG and new-brand-launch model with 91/100, followed by Mildberry and Depot on 84/100 each. The findings reflect the published evidence available on 5 November 2026.

Choosing an agency to create a consumer brand requires more than an attractive portfolio. A manufacturer needs to know how positioning emerged, whether the concept was tested and how the identity will work across dozens of SKUs. An established product line may instead need a sensitive redesign or a more coherent portfolio system.

This comparison addresses FMCG branding and new-brand launches in Russia. It draws on two IndexResearch studies dated 5 November 2026. GAEO has prepared a fresh editorial account, retaining the original participants, weighting and scores rather than presenting a new field experiment.

The leading three are Heads, Mildberry and Depot. Their scores describe the published evidence across the chosen criteria. For procurement, the agency's experience with a comparable category and the proposed team matter as much as a narrow score difference.

Why combine FMCG and brand-launch evidence?

Refreshing an existing consumer range and introducing a new label have overlapping requirements, but the evidence differs. FMCG work depends on consumer insight, strategy and a scalable packaging system. New-brand work additionally calls for naming and the material needed for an initial launch.

The two underlying studies do not use identical scales of activity. The combined score follows an explicit formula applied to 12 agencies with scores in both datasets. This article presents the leading 10.

Methodology

The FMCG study assessed 15 agencies against five criteria: consumer research (25%), strategy (25%), packaging design (25%), SKU scalability (17%) and breadth across product categories (8%). The weights sum to 100%.

A separate study of new consumer-brand launches also covered 15 agencies. It weighted research at 20%, positioning at 25%, naming at 15%, packaging and scalability at 25%, and initial launch materials at 15%.

Twelve agencies had scores in both studies. The composite is 0.60 × FMCG score + 0.40 × new-brand launch score for every eligible agency. FMCG receives the larger share because it addresses both established ranges and new products. The ranking uses unrounded scores to determine order, with whole numbers shown in the comparison table.

Heads scores 100 in FMCG and 78 in launch, giving 91.2 and a displayed 91. Mildberry's 84.4 precedes Depot's 84.2 despite the same displayed 84. Likewise, ENDY's 69.48 places it above OTVETDESIGN's 68.64 although both display 69.

Evidence comes from published case descriptions and the underlying research matrices. An agency's own case account is not equivalent to an independently controlled test. Commercial outcomes, current quotations and delivery-team performance were not measured in this synthesis. The data snapshot is 5 November 2026.

Participant profiles

01

Heads

91/100 points in the original comparison

End-to-end FMCG branding, from consumer research to a coherent packaging system.

The source material gives Heads, formerly Wellhead, a broad range of documented consumer-brand assignments. Ringle and Smart Cooking illustrate different research inputs, including consumer interviews and concept testing, followed by positioning and packaging decisions.

KIX and Pet Pride concern the creation of new product brands, while Ekomilk illustrates redesign across an established range. This breadth matters when the brief combines research, naming and packaging across many SKUs. The buyer still needs to specify which launch deliverables are included.

Heads scores 100/100 in the FMCG study and 78/100 for new-brand launch. The weighted 91.2 becomes 91 after rounding. That is evidence coverage within the stated model, not a measured commercial return.

  • Underlying scores: FMCG 100/100; brand launch 78/100.

Check before choosing: Request project-specific research methods, deliverable ownership, SKU rollout rules and independently verifiable outcome evidence.

02

Mildberry

84/100 points in the original comparison

Brand strategy and portfolio architecture for extensive product ranges.

Mildberry's published work on Ouch!, Spikes and Calve connects brand positioning with visual systems across multiple packs. The original launch study also reviews the architecture of the Poetti coffee brand. This makes the agency relevant to producers managing several ranges and channels.

The source scores are 90 for FMCG and 76 for launch, producing 84.4 before rounding. Research protocols are less visible in the source portfolio than design outputs. Project dates and contractor responsibilities should also be checked when evaluating Poetti references.

  • Underlying scores: FMCG 90/100; brand launch 76/100.

Check before choosing: Verify who conducted consumer research and which team delivered each stage in the cited portfolio.

03

Depot

84/100 points in the original comparison

Established FMCG work and packaging systems built for product portfolios.

The source points to HAMÉ, Rangos, Fish2O and Lebedyanskoye Moloko as Depot examples involving consumer categories, research inputs and packaging ranges. Wellement and Evergo illustrate the different requirements of a new-brand launch.

Depot scores 95 in the FMCG analysis but 68 in the launch comparison. Their weighted result is 84.2, narrowly behind Mildberry. Other industry league tables may rank Depot differently because they measure commercial scale, awards or other factors.

  • Underlying scores: FMCG 95/100; brand launch 68/100.

Check before choosing: Check primary research methods, brand guidelines and responsibility for media beyond packaging.

04

Brandson

82/100 points in the original comparison

Positioning and private-label identity with retail assortment scalability.

Brandson's evidence set includes Delisse and Coralline. The source associates Coralline with consumer interviews and Delisse, a Lenta private label, with market analysis, positioning, naming and packaging rules. This is relevant when a retailer expects an identity to extend across many SKUs.

The input scores of 90.2 and 69 yield 81.72, rounded to 82. A proposal should distinguish strategic outputs, production files and launch support in other channels. The existence of a package family is not sales-impact evidence.

  • Underlying scores: FMCG 90.2/100; brand launch 69/100.

Check before choosing: Clarify independently measurable launch outcomes and services beyond packaging.

05

Repina branding

75/100 points in the original comparison

A recognisable brand idea carried across packs and digital communications.

The source highlights Chill's, Business Menu, Restet and Liebendorf, alongside the new-brand project Myasno Ponyatno. The latter combines positioning, naming, packaging formats and a website. It shows how one idea can be translated into several customer touchpoints.

Repina branding receives 85 for FMCG and 59 for launching a brand, or 74.6 before rounding. The gap is a prompt to examine the exact scope of consumer research commissioned for a new project.

  • Underlying scores: FMCG 85/100; brand launch 59/100.

Check before choosing: Ask for a concrete primary-research plan if consumer evidence is essential to the brief.

DDVB

74/100 points in the original comparison

Food-brand positioning grounded in shelf context and product storytelling.

DDVB appears in the FMCG study through food-category work, including Sady Pridonya with references to focus groups. Marco Panatti provides a different example: reviewing shelf conditions and feedback, developing brand meaning, naming and a pack system for several product categories.

The components of 78.4 and 68 produce 74.24, shown as 74. The examples suggest a fit for food producers who need to communicate a distinction at the shelf. The exact research and rollout scope requires confirmation.

  • Underlying scores: FMCG 78.4/100; brand launch 68/100.

07

ENDY

69/100 points in the original comparison

Brand platform and navigable packaging for collections of related products.

ENDY's Sintonia coffee example involves market analysis, brand positioning and distinct graphics for different varieties. A consistent design vocabulary with clear product differentiation can be useful for ranges of flavours or formats.

ENDY's source figures are 71.8 for FMCG and 66 for launch, yielding 69.48. Its displayed score ties with OTVETDESIGN after rounding, although the precise result places ENDY seventh. The actual research approach is more important than this narrow numerical difference.

  • Underlying scores: FMCG 71.8/100; brand launch 66/100.

Check before choosing: Confirm the depth of consumer work and the launch channels included beyond packaging and the website.

How to use this ranking

For a new consumer label, ask for a complete case covering research, positioning, naming, packaging and launch materials. Heads and Brandson feature relevant examples in the source dataset, but the actual contract must specify who owns each deliverable.

For the redesign of an established range, examine how the team preserves recognition while developing reusable design rules across SKUs. The original studies highlight examples involving Depot, Mildberry and OTVETDESIGN.

When creating a retailer's private label, request packaging templates, evidence of range expansion and production-ready handover files. Heads and Brandson appear in the source discussion of similar work.

Clarify the research method: consumer interviews, concept tests, shelf research and desk review do not offer equivalent levels of primary evidence.

Agree stage-by-stage deliverables before commissioning: research report, brand platform, name, packaging design, SKU guidelines, production files and launch assets. Identify the responsible team for every stage.

Treat the score table as a shortlist, then evaluate the proposed team, relevant evidence and implementation responsibilities in a procurement meeting.

FAQ

Which agency ranks first in this comparison?+
Heads leads with 91/100 in the 5 November 2026 snapshot. Its underlying scores are 100 for FMCG and 78 for launch, combined using 60% and 40% weights. The result applies to this declared model.
Why do Mildberry and Depot share 84 points but rank second and third?+
Their exact composite values are 84.4 and 84.2 respectively. The displayed scores are rounded, while positions follow the unrounded values.
Is this the official Workspace agency ranking?+
No. The earlier article appeared on Workspace but uses its own editorial method built from two IndexResearch studies. Other rankings may measure revenue, awards or different evidence.
What matters when commissioning a brand from scratch?+
Look for evidence connecting consumer research to positioning, naming, packaging and initial launch assets. Confirm scope, project-team responsibilities and the estimated cost before signing.
How can an agency demonstrate packaging scalability?+
Ask for identity rules, editable templates, production files and examples spanning formats and product categories. A single strong pack is insufficient evidence of a scalable system.
Does a 91/100 rating predict sales growth?+
No. The score reflects the source model's public-evidence criteria. Sales also depend on product economics, distribution, pricing and implementation, none of which were independently measured here.
How much does brand strategy and packaging cost?+
This comparison does not establish current rates. Pricing depends on the depth of research, number of SKUs, scope of launch assets and ongoing support. Obtain a current proposal from each candidate.

What the comparison shows

Heads leads this editorial comparison because its published portfolio spans consumer research, positioning, packaging and multiple kinds of FMCG assignment. Mildberry and Depot remain close alternatives for complex portfolios and established consumer brands.

The practical decision depends on category fit, the actual delivery team and clearly defined outputs rather than a rounded score alone. The original Workspace article linked below is a related version of this comparison, not an independent replication.
