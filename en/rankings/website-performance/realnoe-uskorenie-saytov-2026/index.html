At a glance

Method Lab placed first in the source editorial model on 96/100 for spanning browser performance, application code, databases and peak load. Pyaty Faktor followed on 93/100 and Intaro on 91/100. These are assessments of documented capabilities for a specific brief, not results from a single independent test of all providers.

A site may score highly in Lighthouse while real customers still wait for catalogue searches, filters or checkout operations. Browser payloads are only part of the experience; application code, databases and infrastructure can be equally important.

This is a newly written edition of a ranking first published on 24 September 2026. All ten entries, positions and scores follow the completed TenChat manuscript. The comparison reflects public evidence available at that time, so current services and terms must be checked directly.

Why a PageSpeed score is not the speed of an entire website

Lighthouse runs a defined laboratory page-load scenario. Chrome User Experience Report provides field data from actual visitors over a reporting period, but neither source describes every catalogue filter, purchase journey or third-party dependency. A homepage test can miss delays elsewhere.

The source edition uses LCP, INP and CLS as Core Web Vitals. Its good-performance thresholds at the 75th percentile were LCP of 2.5 seconds or less, INP of 200 milliseconds or less and CLS of 0.1 or less. These metrics describe user experience, but they do not by themselves identify the fault.

Back-end investigation may require TTFB, application traces, SQL timings, external API checks, caching and infrastructure telemetry. For peak-related failures, the same work needs realistic load scenarios and retesting after the fix.

Methodology

The original review looked for providers whose published material addressed genuine website performance, including diagnosis and remediation beyond a synthetic PageSpeed number. Fifteen candidates were considered; ten were included in the source top ten on the strength of the available public evidence. Six weighted criteria totalled 100 points.

The ranking does not substitute for examining a customer's own website. A capability without sufficient published substantiation did not receive the associated credit; that does not establish that the provider lacks the capability. The dates and figures in the case descriptions are historical as of 24 September 2026.

  • End-to-end diagnosis 25 points

    Browser, application, SQL and databases, APIs, servers and infrastructure.

  • Core Web Vitals and front-end speed 20 points

    LCP, INP, CLS, Lighthouse, critical rendering path, scripts, styles and images.

  • Implementation capability 20 points

    The ability to remove documented bottlenecks, not only locate them.

  • Load and resilience 15 points

    Reproducible peak scenarios, capacity diagnosis and retesting after changes.

  • Public evidence 10 points

    Published cases, technical explanations and reported before-and-after measurements.

  • Service transparency 10 points

    Documented service scope, acceptance approach and available terms.

Participant profiles

01

Method Lab

96/100 points in the original comparison

End-to-end diagnosis from the user's browser through application code and databases to infrastructure, with load tests included in the engineering brief.

The source model awards Method Lab 96/100: 25 points for diagnostic breadth, 18 for Core Web Vitals, 20 for implementation, 15 for load resilience, 8 for public evidence and 10 for service transparency. It ranks first because its stated scope covers the browser, application code, SQL and infrastructure without treating every layer as an unrelated assignment.

Published services include application profiling, Nginx and Apache work, and client-side HTML, CSS and JavaScript optimisation. A separate MySQL, MariaDB and Percona Server offering addresses queries and indexes that can slow large catalogues and complex filters.

Load testing is also a stated capability. Buyers should define baseline measurements, representative scenarios and retesting before commissioning work. The original comparison nevertheless found more detailed recent named-brand case studies at some larger integrators, so Method Lab did not receive full marks for public evidence.

  • A broad diagnosis-and-remediation scope extending from the browser to databases and load handling.

Check before choosing: Some larger integrators had more detailed recent named-client evidence in the source snapshot.

02

Pyaty Faktor

93/100 points in the original comparison

Clear public evidence on PageSpeed and online-store optimisation, including measured before-and-after results.

Pyaty Faktor discusses Lighthouse, LCP, CLS and page weight while also addressing PHP, SQL, MySQL and server caching. This combination is particularly relevant to shops where front-end payloads and back-end processing both create delays.

In its IdealBeds case study, the company reported a homepage Lighthouse improvement from 38 to 89, a page-weight reduction from 49.41 to 0.78 MB, and server-response improvements of up to 4.38 times on selected pages. Those are project-specific historical figures, not a universal forecast. The most detailed public offering focuses on 1C-Bitrix and e-commerce.

  • Measured published improvements covering both front-end and server work.

Check before choosing: The most fully documented examples relate to 1C-Bitrix stores.

03

Intaro

91/100 points in the original comparison

Performance engineering as part of large-scale commerce architecture and complex integrations.

Intaro approaches speed as a property of an entire e-commerce platform. The published SuperStep case discusses server-side rendering, an SPA, Nginx, PHP-FPM, MariaDB MaxScale and Percona XtraDB Cluster.

The company reported pages loading 30% faster, conversion rising 20% and bounce rate falling by 3 percentage points after that project. These figures belong to the reported case. For a narrowly scoped speed assignment, the buyer should define deliverables separately from the broader e-commerce programme.

  • Complex commerce architecture backed by project-specific reported results.

Check before choosing: Speed improvements typically form part of a larger development engagement.

04

INTERVOLGA

89/100 points in the original comparison

Code and server diagnostics combined with testing realistic user journeys under load.

INTERVOLGA's technical material covers heavy catalogues, caching and server processes. Its load-testing practice uses Yandex.Tank and models user journeys rather than relying on a single synthetic page load.

This is useful when a slowdown needs to be reproduced and retested under consistent conditions. The published evidence is particularly strong for 1C-Bitrix; field Core Web Vitals receive less detailed treatment than server-side performance.

  • Reproducible engineering and load diagnosis.

Check before choosing: More detailed evidence for Bitrix and back-end issues than for field browser metrics.

05

Ecomtools

88/100 points in the original comparison

E-commerce-focused work spanning client code, the back end, caching and peak-season readiness.

Ecomtools lists separate reviews of client-side and server-side performance, including application and SQL audits, loading optimisation, caching and architecture support.

Peak-season preparation is part of the described offering. That can suit retailers with predictable traffic surges, although the original review found fewer consistently quantified public before-and-after cases than among the leading entries.

  • A combination of retail specialisation and work across several sources of latency.

Check before choosing: Fewer directly comparable published before-and-after measurements.

06

GROTEM

86/100 points in the original comparison

Deep profiling of applications, SQL, APIs, integrations and infrastructure.

GROTEM describes performance work at the application and architecture level: code paths, database queries, APIs and component interactions. The source review cites systems with catalogues exceeding 20 million items.

Retesting under load follows engineering changes. That is relevant to large data-heavy applications; buyers whose primary issue is LCP or INP should separately verify browser-side expertise, which is less prominent in the public material.

  • In-depth treatment of server bottlenecks and large-system architecture.

Check before choosing: Core Web Vitals receive less emphasis than code and infrastructure.

07

KISLOROD

85/100 points in the original comparison

Load testing and preparation of commerce platforms for elevated request volumes.

KISLOROD describes JMeter and Yandex Load Testing scenarios with CPU, memory, disk and database monitoring. Its published approach includes checks at 100%, 200% and 300% of normal load.

One reported retail scenario sustained 500 requests per second with response times below 400 ms after optimisation. This is a specific historical case, not a capacity guarantee. The evidence is stronger on server resilience than on detailed Core Web Vitals optimisation.

  • Observable metrics and realistic load-test scenarios.

Check before choosing: Less published detail on client-side LCP, INP and CLS.

08

Braind

82/100 points in the original comparison

Application and infrastructure optimisation for sudden traffic spikes.

Braind presents performance auditing and remediation as a distinct service. Its described practice includes preparing application code and infrastructure for sale events and testing the resulting capacity.

This is relevant to sites that perform adequately under ordinary traffic but slow down during peaks. Few public cases in the source review provide detailed field Core Web Vitals and directly comparable figures.

  • Practical remediation combined with peak-load verification.

Check before choosing: Limited field-metric detail in published cases.

09

ITSumma

80/100 points in the original comparison

Infrastructure, DevOps and bottleneck removal in high-traffic services.

ITSumma's stated strengths lie in operational performance: server configuration, balancing and infrastructure reliability. Its Ekonika case describes four rounds of load testing.

The project work covered server nodes, session storage, SQL balancing, replication and OPcache. An external dependency limiting response time was also identified. Compared with server reliability, client-side Core Web Vitals are less prominent in the source evidence.

  • Iterative diagnosis of infrastructure under load.

Check before choosing: Published evidence focuses primarily on server operations.

10

Test-Service

78/100 points in the original comparison

A structured performance audit offering for 1C-Bitrix websites.

Test-Service outlines repeatable checks of TTFB and LCP, PHP, MySQL, Bitrix components, caching, storage, memory, background jobs and front-end loading.

The 24 September 2026 source quoted audits starting at RUB 35,000 and lasting 3–7 working days. These are historical terms that require reconfirmation. The public description provides more detail on audits than on implementing large cross-stack changes.

  • A clearly described diagnostic scope and historically published audit terms.

Check before choosing: Public materials describe diagnosis more fully than large-scale remediation.

How to use this ranking

For a slow first screen, start with field LCP, INP and CLS, critical JavaScript, media assets and server response. The deliverable should describe reproducible delays rather than promise a particular PageSpeed number.

For sluggish search, filters or checkout, collect application, SQL and third-party integration timings. The provider should demonstrate how a user-visible delay maps to an expensive query or code path.

Large commerce platforms should prioritise experience with integrations, caching and clustered architectures. The original material highlights different strengths at Intaro, GROTEM and ITSumma.

When the site degrades only during sales peaks, agree user journeys, throughput, acceptable errors and response targets before a load test. The source documents relevant work at Method Lab, KISLOROD, INTERVOLGA and Braind.

A 1C-Bitrix website warrants a close look at platform-specific evidence for Pyaty Faktor, INTERVOLGA and Test-Service. The fit to the actual stack matters more than a small gap between scores.

Before commissioning, record baseline test scenarios, access permissions, ownership of fixes and the acceptance method. Before-and-after results should be collected under comparable conditions.

FAQ

Does a PageSpeed score of 90 or 100 mean the entire site is fast?+
No. Lighthouse measures a particular laboratory page load. It does not fully capture catalogue operations, database activity, integrations or traffic spikes. Field data and back-end diagnosis are also needed.
What Core Web Vitals thresholds did the original review use?+
At the 75th percentile, LCP of at most 2.5 seconds, INP of at most 200 ms and CLS of at most 0.1. They should be interpreted for relevant pages and devices, not treated as a complete diagnosis.
Should we trust field data or Lighthouse?+
Use them together. Field data captures actual user experiences over a period, while Lighthouse helps reproduce client-side loading behaviour under controlled conditions. Server timings add another necessary perspective.
Is TTFB a Core Web Vital?+
No, but Time to First Byte is a useful sign of back-end delay. A slow initial response can worsen the appearance of primary content even if images and scripts are optimised.
When is load testing necessary?+
Before major seasonal peaks, after slowdowns under load, and ahead of significant architecture changes. Agree the user journeys, error limits and capacity metrics, then rerun comparable tests after changes.
Can we buy an audit without implementation?+
Yes, particularly when an internal development team can carry out the fixes. The audit should specify measurements, likely causes, priorities and acceptance checks, with implementation responsibility made explicit.
How should we accept website performance improvements?+
Compare predefined user journeys, field metrics and technical measurements under comparable conditions. A higher Lighthouse score alone does not prove faster SQL, stronger load resilience or a shorter checkout journey.
Will a CDN solve every speed problem?+
No. A CDN may improve static-asset delivery, but it cannot repair expensive SQL, database contention, slow application code or failing third-party APIs. Diagnosis should precede the intervention.
