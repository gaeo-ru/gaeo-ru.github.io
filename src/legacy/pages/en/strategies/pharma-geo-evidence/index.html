Pharmaceutical GEO: brand visibility and medical accuracy in AI answers

Alexey YakovlevPublished: October 27, 2026Updated: October 27, 2026Industry methodology

Pharmaceutical GEO requires accurate medical information, traceable sources and transparent measurement.

Pharmaceutical AI answers can recognise a brand and still make a material mistake about its ingredient, indications or limitations. As a GEO researcher at GAEO.ru, I review these questions; the commercial scope appears under GEO for pharmaceutical brands.

This is a framework for AI-answer analysis using an existing anonymized August 2026 audit and official sources. It contains no patient treatment recommendations. Medical claims require qualified review before client publication.

1. Eight categories of pharmaceutical information needs

The following are illustrative research categories, not verbatim private client prompts. They allow researchers to compare like-for-like answers and verify the right source for each claim.

Medicines, devices and dietary supplements should be treated as separate regulated product categories.

2. Authoritative registries and appropriate markets

For medicines in Russia, consult the State Register of Medicines and the currently approved labeling. The clinical recommendations portal provides a distinct type of evidence with its own medical scope and revision history. The Roszdravnadzor register supports device-identity and intended-use checks.

Other jurisdictions require their own primary registration sources. EMA's medicine finder covers centrally evaluated EU medicines; nationally authorised products require national-register checks.

3. Three different visibility observations

Code literal trade-name presence, INN mentions and active contextual recommendations as distinct outcomes. A description of an ingredient does not establish recognition of its brand. Log citations and any material factual concerns separately.

Unbranded BMR equals answers with the brand's literal trade name divided by all obtained answers to the unchanged unbranded prompt set. Branded questions are excluded from that denominator and used for factual QA. See the BMR methodology.

4. An anonymized 120-answer baseline

The previously published GAEO pharmaceutical audit ran on August 6–9, 2026: 20 fixed questions across 6 interfaces and 120 complete responses. Fifteen unbranded questions produced 90 answers; 5 branded questions produced 30 additional responses used to check accuracy.

The diagnostic record logged 16 material risks or inaccuracies across 14 unique answers, including 2 related-information concerns. The medicine and manufacturer remain anonymous. There is no documented comparable follow-up or demonstrated GEO growth in the available materials.

5. Reviewing medical errors and pharmacovigilance

Every suspected error needs a preserved answer, source, date, issue priority, qualified reviewer and remediation status. Check indications, contraindications, patient groups and dispensing restrictions first. The client's medical professionals approve all factual corrections.

Potential adverse events must follow the organisation's pharmacovigilance procedures. WHO defines pharmacovigilance through detecting, assessing, understanding and preventing adverse reactions and related problems.

6. Russian advertising requirements

Article 24 of Russian Advertising Law No. 38-FZ sets specific restrictions for medicine and medical-device promotion. Advertising must not guarantee the absence of adverse effects, safety or effectiveness. Prescription products have channel restrictions, and relevant advertising must comply with applicable warning requirements.

Legal status depends on content and distribution. Educational, professional and promotional communications require appropriate legal and medical review. GEO does not override these requirements.

7. Website content and data consistency

Connect the provider's verified identity, trade name, INN, authorisation and approved product documentation. Separate corporate news, trials and clinical recommendations by date and provenance. Corrections need assigned responsibility.

Schema.org should represent the content actually visible. Google Search Central places particular emphasis on accuracy and trust for health information. GAEO prepares technical research; qualified client professionals verify medical claims.

8. What the customer receives

  1. Fixed branded and unbranded question inventory with intended audience.
  2. Complete responses, observation dates and original source links.
  3. Separate mention, recommendation, factual-error and missing-response indicators.
  4. Risk register with severity and qualified medical reviewer.
  5. Action plan for appropriately approved website and external content changes.
  6. Like-for-like follow-up measurements after implemented corrections.

New products, languages and markets need their own baselines. An increase in BMR alone does not establish a better or safer pharmaceutical GEO outcome.
